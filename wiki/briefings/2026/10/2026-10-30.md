## Retrospective addendum — 2026-11-02

*Added 2026-11-02 under issue #2, after every project this briefing covers was deepened to source level. The snapshot above is unaltered; this addendum records what verification did to its claims.*

One framing fact: the briefing made no claim about any covered project beyond listing it; its only interpretive claim is the frontier signal, "Trustworthy evidence before accumulating memory." The verdicts below attach to the selection and to the summary-level characterizations the import carried (whose metrics were previously unverified).

### What held

- **VeriCUA** — the core claim held: a separate post-hoc verifier, independent of the executing agent's own completion claims, beats judges and generated verifiers at checking computer-use task completion. On OSReward-Hard it is the only compared method improving on both metrics from flash to pro (accuracy 70.4 → 73.1, balanced accuracy 68.3 → 69.2), and online it posts the highest accuracy and lowest false-positive rate in all three environments — AndroidWorld accuracy 90.5 with FPR 7.7. It rejects about 80% of false completion reports, and its ternary verdict — success, fail, or abstain — makes insufficient evidence an accounted outcome rather than a forced call: [[concepts/abstention-as-first-class-verdict]].
- **ExploreRepair** — the information-constrained setting is real, and the system works inside it. Only 4.1% of 75,807 CVEs had a public proof-of-vulnerability within a year of disclosure (Householder et al. 2020, as cited). ExploreRepair receives only the repository and a function-level hint, builds its own PoV, and tops PatchEval at 39.6% (GPT-5.6-Luna) and 71.6% (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash) repair success, against 32.5 / 57.4 for a single agent and 30.2 / 55.6 for generic multi-agent delegation — which underperforms even the lone agent. Aggregation creates repairs, not just selects them: the full system repairs 11 vulnerabilities (DeepSeek) and 3 (Luna) none of its explorers repaired individually.
- **ExLabel** — confirmed as an automated assessor of SWE-bench-style instances on both axes it claims: task clarity and test-suite adequacy, judged against an experience bank that evolves from annotated instances. It achieves the best F1 in all 12 combinations of task, subset, and backbone, with average relative F1 improvements of +44.2% and +49.9% on clarity (Python, Multilingual) and +24.0% and +18.4% on coverage over the best baseline. The bank is load-bearing, not decoration: removing it drops F1 by 25.5–35.7% on clarity and 49.3–84.0% on coverage. Its labels pay downstream: filtering training data with them yields 66.2% resolved on SWE-bench Verified against 57.8% for the best baseline filter.
- **CodeSpecWeave** — confirmed, and its most consequential figure stands exactly as reported: in a pilot generation of 3,067 candidate tasks, 2,267 — 73.9% — carried at least one test-alignment flaw. Tests for interdependent specifications cannot be validated by inspection; the framework's answer is an executed, trajectory-audited verification loop. The output is real: 4,670 training tasks across 2,865 repositories, and a 100-task benchmark carrying 1,044 specifications (mean 10.44 per task). Training on its tasks yields the highest four-benchmark average on both backbones tested, with the largest single gain on DeepSWE-v1.1 for MiMo-V2.5, 12.39% → 25.30%.
- **A2UI and CopilotKit** — the structure is confirmed in every checkable particular. The protocol defines exactly four message types (`createSurface`, `updateComponents`, `updateDataModel`, `deleteSurface`); the Basic Catalog holds 18 components and 14 named functions, cross-checked between the Google blog and the specification; v0.9.1 is the current production release, with v1.0 only a candidate. The agent composes declarative JSON from a catalog the client declared in advance, the client's own native components render it, and no executable code crosses the boundary. Since v0.9, generation is prompt-first — free JSON validated after the fact, with a standardized `VALIDATION_FAILED` error returned to the model for self-correction.
- **Auctopus** — the end-to-end investigation is confirmed, including its most unusual event. The system proved an exact calibration law, d_L = L² + 1: at L = 3 the required calibration count falls from the proposed bound of 91 to 10. Then the system rejected its own first experimental run: it met all ten precommitted accuracy criteria, but the global-phase and reference-pulse controls failed (the signal rose 7.6% over the morning, with a further 2.9% step at 11:48 a.m.), so it was classified inconclusive and the protocol revised without relaxing the criteria. The revised run passed everything: measured contrast S = (40.0 ± 0.4)×10⁻³ against the locked prediction P = (39.7 ± 1.3)×10⁻³, where the interpolation alternatives predicted exactly 0.
- **Repairs for Whom?** — the central prescription held under pre-specified, Holm-confirmed tests: repair selection must match the deployed executor, not the strongest available judge. At equal rollout budget, selection by the stronger same-family Qwen2.5-14B for the 7B executor loses 0.25 [0.19, 0.32] of net gain in ALFWorld and 0.07 [0.05, 0.10] in WebShop; even greedy replay of its own trajectory loses 0.08–0.13. Labels are executor-relative: the repair-location argmax from 14B rollouts agrees with the deployed agent's in only 32% [26, 38] of ALFWorld trajectories, against 62% agreement between disjoint halves of its own rollouts — [[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]].
- **Security Impact of Coding Agent Memory Files** — the channel held end-to-end. A planted project memory file (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md) is auto-loaded at session start and reaches the agent as trusted project guidance: an indirect prompt-injection entry point, demonstrated rather than merely asserted. The planted files are crafted to be invisible as attacks — they never name a CWE and avoid the words "security" and "attack" entirely, phrasing defensive patterns as anti-patterns in ordinary engineering register. Functionality stayed unmoved — plausible completions held between 80 and 92 percent in every mode — so any steering would have remained invisible behind passing tests.

### What was corrected or qualified

- **VeriCUA** — "independent" verification → independent of the executing agent only. Six of the eight authors are Google researchers; Google preview models serve as verifier, judge, and executing-agent backbones; and two of the five benchmarks — OSReward and ScienceBoard, the settings with the most dramatic margins — come from the same research group as the method itself. The baselines are also the authors' re-implementations, two of them reduced: CUAJudge's pipeline collapsed into one call, OS-Themis cut from six milestones and twelve frames to four and eight (restoring its budget raises its balanced accuracy from 74.2 to 75.9). Even the gold labels cut both ways (51 of 81 flagged contradictions adjudicated in VeriCUA's favor): [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]], [[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]], [[concepts/scoped-assurance]].
- **ExploreRepair** — the headline margins → measured against adapted, weakened baselines. EvoRepair, the principal comparator, is designed around a ground-truth PoV and oracle feedback; here it received neither, and its loop was cut from 15 turns to 3. The fairer comparison is VulnLoc+ against LoopRepair with its PoV intact, where the margin narrows from +20.0 / +17.1 points to +2.9 / +11.4 — and the no-PoV variant is the authors' minimal construction, selecting the first patch that compiles. VulnLoc+ success is also PoV-only, with no unit-test or overfitting check. The evaluation sets are small and filtered (PatchEval 169 of 230; VulnLoc+ 35 of 40), with no confidence intervals and no reported run cost. Aggregation can destroy correct work: in the Synapse case (CVE-2021-39163), both aggregators adopted the same wrong assumption and the final stage discarded the Graph agent's correct repair — [[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]].
- **ExLabel** — a neutral quality benchmark → SWE-TQ was constructed and cleaned by the same team that built the assessor, around the very failure modes ExLabel targets. Its ground truth retains only instances all annotators already agreed on — the least ambiguous cases — so performance on contested instances, where automated assessment would be most valuable, is unmeasured. The cleaning removed flagged instances (34 of 148 Python and 136 of 384 Multilingual instances matching the two known error patterns) rather than relabeling them, shaping both the accumulation pool and the test labels. The absolute numbers are more modest than the relative gains suggest: Multilingual clarity F1 is 25.4–37.5, on positive rates of only 4.5–6.0%. Ablations run on a single backbone, with no variance reporting: [[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]].
- **CodeSpecWeave** — verified benchmark figures → every number is author-reported, the benchmark's tasks were manually reviewed by the authors themselves, and nothing is independently replicated. The deeper qualification is structural: the same pipeline and the same MiMo construction agents produce both the 4,670-task training set and the 100-task benchmark, so part of the on-benchmark gain may reflect train/test distribution match rather than transferable capability — and the paper does not describe how overlap was excluded. Nor are the wins uniform: on the MiMo-V2.5 backbone, the MiMo Coding baseline beats the CodeSpecWeave-trained model on SWE-bench Verified (73.63 vs. 72.00) and SWE-bench Pro (53.81 vs. 51.30). The gains coincide with 3.0–4.8× more thinking tokens after training, with no compute-matched control: [[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]], [[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]].
- **A2UI and CopilotKit** — one product with demonstrated benefits → two artifacts with different owners, and no measured benefits at all. A2UI is Google's protocol specification; CopilotKit is a separate framework, and its AG-UI protocol is a transport, not part of the specification. Across all primary sources there is no quantitative result of any kind: no validity rate, no latency figure, no user study. Every benefit — invalid UI "caught before it reaches the screen," "self-healing output," native rendering across six frameworks — is an unquantified vendor claim. Renderer coverage is thinner than the claim suggests (the README lists Web or Flutter as current), and the security boundary is structural, not total: a manipulated agent can assemble a phishing-shaped interface from trusted components. The mechanism's decisive property — how often models produce valid, appropriate UI — has no published number.
- **Auctopus** — predictions committed before the experiment → the prediction lock came after data collection, before any target mean was computed (Methods 7.6). That is analysis-order discipline — a real safeguard, weaker than pre-registration: the data already existed when the predictions were locked. The acceptance criteria were likewise self-set and self-graded — fixed by the system's own PI agent, never externally registered or independently justified. The internal checks are process separation within one framework, not independent replication. The decisive rivals were weak by construction: both interpolation baselines predict S = 0 for any calibration record. Even the first run's drift diagnosis is the experimentalist agent's own inference, timing-corroborated, not audited: [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]], [[concepts/scoped-assurance]].
- **Repairs for Whom?** — pre-registered confirmation → the analysis plan was dated and pre-specified, but never independently timestamped or publicly registered; its confirmatory status rests on the authors' released, anonymized internal record. The published methods it beats — DoVer's 2-of-3 rule, CausalFlow's single-replay flip, AgenTracer's earliest success, Causal Agent Replay's latest significant step — appear only as re-implemented verification steps inside this fixed-candidate protocol, not as full pipelines, so the losses attach to the rules in isolation. Further qualifications accumulate: the screened pools' interval re-centering was added after the pre-specified analyses ran; the WebShop objective was switched from success to reward after a pilot; the human study uses a single annotator who is a co-author. Scope is narrow — two open 7–8B models, three restorable text environments, single-step repairs: [[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]].
- **Security Impact of Coding Agent Memory Files** — memory files steer agents toward vulnerable code → the steering implication collapses under the study's own numbers. Secure-completion shifts were −8 to +6 percentage points with no consistent pattern; four of the six shifts were *increases*, and every shift sits inside the band that cannot be told from chance. The paired analysis is exactly chance-shaped: 30 discordant pairs split 15/15 between baseline-secure and file-secure. Recognition did not protect the code either: of 18 sessions whose reasoning called the file an attack, only 4 named it in the final message, and security was lost in 3, gained in 2. The design also favors the attacker — a bespoke file per CWE class — and it has no benign-file control: without one, an instruction file's mere presence cannot be separated from its malicious content. The entry point is demonstrated end-to-end; reliable steering is not: [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]].

### What the briefing could not yet see

- **The day's verifiers graded on home ground.** VeriCUA's most dramatic margins sit on same-group benchmarks against reduced re-implementations of its rivals; ExLabel's ground truth is a dataset its own team built and filtered to unanimous agreement; CodeSpecWeave's benchmark tasks were reviewed by the authors and synthesized by the pipeline that made its training data. Visible only when the pages were read side by side: [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]] and [[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]].
- **Reported margins partly measure how the comparator was built.** ExploreRepair's headline is an artifact of a baseline stripped of its PoV, its oracle feedback, and four fifths of its loop; Repairs for Whom? shows rankings under substitute executors correlating only τ ≈ 0.46–0.51 with deployment-relative rankings. A margin is a statement about a pair of systems, not a property of one: [[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]].
- **The load-bearing words were scoped words.** "Independent" survived only as independence from the executing agent; "pre-registered" only as a dated internal plan; "committed" only as locked before the means were computed. The scope, not the word, is the finding: [[concepts/scoped-assurance]].
- **Verification had quietly become an agent loop, not a judge call.** VeriCUA inspects context, synthesizes a rubric, gathers typed evidence, and arbitrates deterministically, with abstention as a first-class outcome. Auctopus rejected a run that passed every accuracy criterion because its controls failed. They are one technique arriving in several places at once: [[concepts/abstention-as-first-class-verdict]].
- **The signal's second half, refined.** The memory results split: ExLabel's gated, curated experience bank is worth 49.3–84.0% of F1 on coverage judgments, while a planted, ungated memory file moved secure completions by a chance-like −8 to +6 points: [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]].
- **Aggregation amplifies whatever the aggregators share — including the error.** ExploreRepair's Synapse case — two aggregators ratifying a shared wrong assumption, discarding a correct repair — is the shadow of VeriCUA's neatest result: rule-based arbitration over the same draws produced 2 wrong fails where majority voting produced 20.

Read today, this briefing is evidence of what a summary-level import preserves and what it loses. The selection survived intact, and the frontier signal pointed at the right pattern. But every quantitative characterization the import carried needed correction in scope, in comparator, or in ground truth, and the day's real structure — circular evaluation, scoped assurance, gated memory — was invisible at summary level. Its value now is as the dated "before" picture, against which the deepened pages are the measurement.
