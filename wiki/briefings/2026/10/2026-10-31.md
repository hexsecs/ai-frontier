## Retrospective addendum — 2026-11-02

*Added 2026-11-02 under issue #2, after every project this briefing covers was deepened to source level. The snapshot above is unaltered; this addendum records what verification did to its claims.*

One framing fact: the briefing made no claim about any covered project beyond listing it; its only interpretive claim is the frontier signal, "Compile learned procedures into tested programs and typed contracts." The verdicts below attach to the selection and the summary-level characterizations the import carried (metrics previously unverified).

### What held

**SkillCompiler** — the design claim confirmed as described. Operations meeting explicit executability criteria become validated, deterministic programs in a persistent Program Asset Bank; the rest stays LLM reasoning, and no model parameters are ever updated. SkillCompiler tops all six model families on the four-benchmark average, beating each model's strongest baseline by 6.02–11.90 points, and reaches 98.26% success on ALFWorld against 93.53% for the best baseline. The ablation identifies the active ingredient — removing the Programmer collapses ALFWorld success from 98.26% to 53.48%. It is now a core instance of [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]].

**WebAnything** — the automation claim held, for exactly the subset it was made about: for the historical 85-site Full Coverage subset, no recorded per-site manual implementation, debugging, or repair after initial configuration and necessary authorization. The fidelity result held as reported — on five sites and 14 states matched against human-assisted WebHarbor, WebAnything wins all three aggregate metrics (Pixel Similarity 0.9240 vs. 0.8083, SSIM 0.7904 vs. 0.6260, Text F1 0.8541 vs. 0.2617), and a blind human audit of the same states gives it 12 wins, 2 ties, 0 losses. Scale: 244 website artifacts, 12,984 executed and accepted tasks, at a recorded model-call cost of $220.62 across 84 sites (a lower bound).

**AgenticDS** — the contract-governed design is confirmed, with a rare cross-check: the public repository's result tables match the paper's Tables 2 and 3. Its Planner, Engineer, and Critic exchange typed plans, deterministic operators, and scored evidence packages — every language-model decision instantiates a typed object before deterministic code consumes it. Retrieval held: NDCG@10 of 0.6148 on HuDS-Bench, +25.7% over the strongest dense–sparse hybrid (0.4893) and +18.9% over the strongest agent baseline (0.5170). The ablations support the design's logic — removing the Critic costs −0.149 NDCG@10, the largest single effect — and its retrieved data posts the best downstream training averages in all three domains (52.81 vs. 49.75).

**Seq@K** — the divergence claim held. Across 26 model–benchmark pairs, Pass@5 and Seq@5 correlate at Spearman +0.68, but the gains (ΔPass@5 vs. ΔSeq@5) correlate at −0.08 once first attempts are removed: how much a model gains from more independent samples says little about how much it gains from feedback. The consequence held too — calibrating with Pass@5 picked actors whose held-out Seq@5 reached only 70.8% (HumanEval Pro) and 90.2% (MBPP Pro), while calibrating with Seq@3, picked actors reaching 96.8% and 98.4%. Seq@K is a founding instance of [[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]].

**Intent Loss in Smart-Contract Synthesis** — both headline findings held. Information obtained before generation dominates everything after it: a fixed ten-question checklist answered before generation adds twenty points of transaction-level fidelity across model families — Claude Sonnet 5 rises from 56.1% to 75.1% (+19.0), Gemini 3.5 Flash from 51.8% to 74.5% (+22.7) — and at scale (499 contracts) the elicitation-first pipeline scores 72.9% against 53.9% for one-shot generation. The grading instruments miss planted errors: Slither caught 1.5% of 200 guard-removal mutants; an LLM judge caught 65.0% of 40 mutants while flagging 80% of untouched contracts; the pipeline's self-grading rubric judge correlated with human raters at r = 0.15. The study sits inside [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]] and [[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]].

**FilingLoop** — the scale and the evolution result both held as reported. The corpus: 380 tasks from 96 companies, 3,012 scorable comments decomposed into 6,099 requirement occurrences, of which 76.5% (4,668) are disclosure changes that must appear in the final filing. Procedural evolution with frozen weights improves Requirement Accuracy from 24.28% (Fixed) to 27.96% (Evolving) — +3.68 points. The decomposition is the informative part: Reviewer discovery barely moves (35.45% → 36.86%), while conditional Filer accuracy rises from 68.50% to 78.86% — the learning teaches the Filer to close out concerns already found, not to find new ones. FilingLoop is an instance of [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]].

**HERA** — the blinded trial ran as described, Protocol and corpus were frozen in advance (committed 17 July 2026; git-tagged 19 August 2026); the expert measured each device first and sealed his conclusion and confidence as SHA-256 hashes before each session. HERA matched the sealed conclusion on both devices, in stacking order and confidence level — device 1 (trilayer) ABA, strong; device 2 (tetralayer) ABAB, strong — with zero pre-conclusion interventions on the tetralayer session and one (a safety concern HERA itself raised) on the trilayer. Expert verdict grades were 1.00 for both full-corpus models against 0.40 for both ablated sessions, while decision grades stayed high in all five sessions. The corpus improved inference and verdict quality, not measurement selection.

**GARDEN** — the transfer claim held on its own terms. One reward program per task family, engineered by a tool-using LLM (gpt-6.1-sol), was selected by a fixed mechanical rule, frozen, and hashed, then applied unchanged to held-out scenarios where policies were trained from scratch over 10 matched seeds — so what transferred is the learning signal itself, not policy parameters. The SMAX mean held-out win rate rises from 51.0% under native shaping to 89.9%; the Overcooked held-out layouts gain +58.2% and +52.5% relative to native; on SMAX it leads both internal controls (84.2% and 79.2%). GARDEN sits in [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]] — the improvement is an external, inspectable artifact — and its untouched learner feeds [[concepts/capability-locus]].

### What was corrected or qualified

**SkillCompiler** — "up to 32.33% improvement" resolves to one best model–benchmark cell: GLM-5.3-Flash on LiveMath, 77.00 vs. 44.67. Some cells are near-ties — Kimi-K2.6 gains only +1.52 points on SearchQA and +0.50 on DocVQA — while gains concentrate on the smallest test sets (LiveMath 100, OfficeQA 102, ALFWorld 134 tasks). Validation is weaker at run time than the word suggests: Program Invocation Success is 88.84%, so roughly one in nine invocations of a validated program still fails. The persistent-bank claim has no ablation behind it — Cross-Skill Reuse of 54.85% rests on per-seed denominators of only 10–14 programs. No code or data is linked; nothing is independently replicable.

**WebAnything** — "244 reconstructed websites" is an inventory count, not a quality count. Quality evidence covers roughly ten of the 244 pool sites; 131 of the 244 received no review beyond an HTTP 200 and a homepage screenshot, and of the 113 reviewed only 70 were without confirmed anomalies. The widest headline gap is the weakest-measured: Historical Text F1 (0.8541 vs. 0.2617) compares source HTML replayed with JavaScript disabled against JavaScript-enabled mirror captures. On interaction validity, WebAnything trails the baseline it beat: capability coverage 75% vs. 80%, all-test pass 63.3% vs. 76.7% against WebHarbor. Of 39 claimed Builder–Verifier discrepancy pairs, only one is strictly candidate-matched.

**AgenticDS** — the benchmark is not neutral ground. HuDS-Bench's relational half, where the advantage roughly doubles (+0.206 and +0.197 NDCG@10), is built from the same HuggingKG the system traverses; the authors acknowledge that "benchmark construction shares a graph with the retrieval engine." The search-to-train result rests on three queries (one per domain, no independent seeds); its code and medicine margins are +0.41 and +0.49 points, and it loses two of nine blocks, both in medicine. The "Claude Code" and "Codex" baselines are the authors' own GLM-5.2 harness configurations, single-shot, not the vendors' products; on MAP their margins do not survive Holm correction (p 0.13–0.45).

**Seq@K** — "feedback helps" survives only with its mode attached. The strongest results come from privileged feedback (rubric verdicts, failed tests with expected outputs, rubric-aware critics) — an oracle tutor, not typical user feedback. On CLBench DKR, the best revision repaired 73.8% of failed criteria under rubric-aware critics, versus 34.4% under binary feedback, 36.8% under rubric-blind critics, and 36.5% for independent retries with no feedback: rubric-blind critique is indistinguishable from no feedback, and binary feedback's edge over resampling (−2.1 pp) has an interval including zero. The divergence claim carries its own qualification — the Δ-correlation's interval [−0.47, +0.31] does not exclude a moderately positive relationship, and each pair's Seq@5 there is its best non-binary configuration, optimistic by construction.

**Intent Loss in Smart-Contract Synthesis** — the title's construct is not the measured one. The paper distinguishes author intent, specification compliance, and implementation fidelity — and measures only the third, agreement with the original contract under exact replay. Earlier summaries — and this page's earlier title — blurred exactly that distinction. The elicitation gains are optimistic by construction: the simulated client answers by reading the original contract — the very artifact fidelity is scored against — so they estimate what this simulator provides, not what a human author would. The oracle numbers shrink under the full denominator — over all 279 planted mistakes the best reference model catches 65.2%, not 87.5% — and most of even that is a conventions shortcut (names-only model: 87.5% of the exposed subset). The original pipeline's security loop subtracts value: given complete requirements, fidelity falls from 95.3% to 82.5% (−12.8 points).

**FilingLoop** — the +3.68-point evolution gain comes with a circularity the summary could not show: one model, gpt-6.1-sol, wears four hats — FilingLoop backbone, grader, the bounded fact service's matcher, and skill editor (it also annotated the annual-report extension). A same-family grader may favor its own outputs' style; the bias's size is unknown. Human–grader agreement is unquantified (mechanical validation "does not establish semantic correctness"), and the reference labels are model-annotated, with no measured agreement rate. One seeded stream: no variance estimate, no confidence interval, no held-out set, and the 6,099 requirement occurrences are not independent observations. No configuration exceeds 5.26% Task Accuracy; Evolving fully resolves 2 of 96 companies. This is the briefing's clearest case of [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]].

**HERA** — "validated against expert judgment" becomes, at source level, agreement with one expert: the senior author, who is also the grader of record. On n = 2 devices, one model, the paper itself states the protocol "reports this agreement rather than accuracy." The grading-reliability check failed its pre-sealed prediction: the blinded second expert's exact agreement was 65% (quadratic-weighted κ = 0.38) against ≥0.70 predicted, though the full-over-ablated ordering survived — at one boundary by a margin of 0.002. The ablation changed more than the corpus (ablated sessions received a different brief and output contract), and full-corpus sessions cost roughly 1.5–2.7× more and about 3× the active hours. Anthropic funded the work while Claude models were evaluated (declared; no role stated).

**GARDEN** — the 5.7–10.7-point advantage over the controls is confounded by unmatched inference compute. Budgets were matched, but the search consumed 155 LLM calls and 15.0M input tokens against the controls' 18 calls and ~3.5M each — roughly 8.6× the calls — with no compute-matched control, so the gap cannot be attributed to evidence navigation alone. One reward search per condition leaves search-level variance unmeasured; every margin rests on a single selected program. The +68.8-point gain (27.3% → 96.1%) sits where native shaping was weakest, while where it was already strong the gain is +9.0 points (74.6% → 83.6%). The evaluation was pilot-informed, not researcher-blind, with two held-out scenarios per family.

### What the briefing could not yet see

Read together, the eight share a structure no summary stated: every improvement lived in state around a frozen model, not in the model. SkillCompiler's validated programs accumulate in a Program Asset Bank; FilingLoop's gains live in evolving Markdown procedures; GARDEN's gain is a frozen, hashed reward program; HERA's advantage is a curated corpus. That is [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]] — and the briefing's frontier signal, "compile learned procedures into tested programs and typed contracts," was an early sighting of it.

Where capability lives, the corpus disagrees with itself. HERA's ablation says the curated corpus is decisive — verdict grades 1.00 with it, 0.40 without, while measurement choices barely move. AgenticDS's ablation says the verifier is decisive — remove the Critic and performance falls below both agent baselines. GARDEN leaves its learner untouched and moves everything into the reward. [[concepts/capability-locus]] records this as an open question, not a synthesis.

Its evaluators repeatedly sit inside the systems they grade: FilingLoop's grader is its backbone, fact matcher, and skill editor; AgenticDS's benchmark shares a knowledge graph with its retrieval engine, and its relevance labels come from the same kind of evidence package its Critic consumes; WebAnything's Builder and Verifier are one model family (gpt-5.6-sol) in separate contexts, and it also judges its downstream benchmark; Intent Loss's simulated client reads the artifact the score is computed against. [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]] collects these — each looks, in isolation, like a reasonable design choice.

Scores turned out protocol-relative in ways summaries never flagged: a Seq@K value detached from its feedback mode does not travel; Intent Loss's headline gap on identical outputs is 19 points under a shared-interface scoring rule and 35 under a fixed required interface; AgenticDS's ranking advantage (NDCG) is not sustained by its binary-relevance metric (MAP) after correction. Headline counts outran their validation tier — 244 artifacts against deep review of ~ten sites, simulator-scored gains, one search per condition, validated programs failing one invocation in nine ([[concepts/scoped-assurance]]). [[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]] and [[concepts/validation-tier-gap]] are the lenses built for these shapes.

### Closing

Read today, this briefing is evidence about summary-level understanding itself. Its selection was good — all eight listings became source-checked pages — and its one interpretive claim, the frontier signal, pointed at a pattern the concept layer later confirmed. But every quantitative characterization it carried had to be re-derived: headlines resolved into best cells, counts into inventories, agreements into single-expert concordance, gains into protocol-bound quantities. Nothing in it was fabricated; almost everything in it was one compression too far. That is why the deepening pass existed: the distance between this page's original lines and the paragraphs above is the measured cost of trusting the summary.
