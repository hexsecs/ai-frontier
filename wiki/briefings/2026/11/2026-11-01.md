## Retrospective addendum — 2026-11-02

*Added 2026-11-02 under issue #2, after every project this briefing covers was deepened to source level. The snapshot above is unaltered; this addendum records what verification did to its claims.*

### What held

- The briefing's only substantive claim — its frontier signal, "prefer scoped, reversible self-improvement with regression gates" — held, and the deepening found it implemented almost literally: **KARMA** retains a patch if and only if validation gain g_t ≥ θ = 0.01, otherwise restoring the pre-edit snapshot; **EvoTeam** decides a provisional team's admission to the library only after execution, and prunes rules that no longer fit; **MoLRSI**'s improvement loop is likewise disciplined by held-out evaluation.
- The discovery list itself: all nine listed works were located and read in full at source level, and none was misidentified. Beyond listing them, the briefing made no per-project claims, so there are no per-project verdicts to record; the qualifications below are what the listing's flat presentation left out.

### What was corrected or qualified

- **All nine items** — every result behind every listing is author-reported from a single source; no independent replication exists for any of them. The index's uniform "Discoveries" presentation did not distinguish evidence tiers.
- **GitHub Copilot Fusion** — its claims remain vendor claims, unverifiable from public sources: the entire evidence base is GitHub's changelog post and product documentation, with no paper, artifact, or inspectable benchmark behind them.
- **EvoTeam** — its figures were verified against v1 of the paper; arXiv lists a v2 revised 2026-10-30 against which the numbers have not been re-checked.
- **MoLRSI** — likewise read at v1 with a v2 outstanding; and its two evidence tiers must travel together — the wet-lab results validate the end workflows, while the self-improvement claim itself rests on computational ablations only.
- **Pre-Crime for Agents** — the name promises more than the study delivers: its narrow claims (last-second, per-call detection of imminent disclosure) are well supported on its own data, but the broader "pre-crime" framing is not supported by its own numbers.
- **EnvOS** — its verification machinery is less independent than the listing implied: the reconstruction checkers are separate code paths by the same team, the external archive challenge was generated by an LLM agent rather than an independent team, and the paper discloses an internal non-replication of the team's own earlier result.
- **KARMA** — no replication is possible from public artifacts: neither code nor data is available, and every baseline was re-implemented by the authors themselves.
- **Prefix Executability** — summary-level descriptions blurred this into an intervention; the contribution is a measurement protocol (a deterministic, judge-free replay scorer), and its one intervention, Dual-Loop, is a probe whose validation advantage failed to replicate in the paper's own τ³-bench transfer audit, with the paired sensitivity analysis returning a null.
- **ClinGeX** — nothing in it is independently reproducible at present: the code is not public, data are available only on request, and the agent-core selection scores exist only as radar plots.
- **DynaRev** — its contents were verified against the source text and its repository's existence confirmed, but the benchmark was not re-executed; verification here means reading, not reproduction.

### What the briefing could not yet see

- The frontier signal has a mechanism, and it recurs: in **KARMA**, **EvoTeam**, and **MoLRSI** the improvement lives outside the model — in gated, revisable external state — the pattern now named [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]].
- **ClinGeX** is an instance of [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]]: DeepSeek-V4 builds the skills, serves as the agent core, and generates the ablation items, while two further models judge — and its strongest evidence, the rule-scored held-out benchmark, is precisely the part that escapes the loop.
- **Prefix Executability** shows that a final-success score is a mixture of clean execution and recovery, so rankings are [[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]]: on BFCL, the same condition reads as 0.74 final success or as trajectories only 5% fully executable, depending on the axis chosen.
- Theme-level verdict: nine items listed flat concealed a single repeated question — where does an agent's improvement live, and who grades it — that became visible only once every item had been read at source level.
