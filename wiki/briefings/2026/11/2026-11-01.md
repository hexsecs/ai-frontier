## Retrospective addendum — 2026-11-02

*Added 2026-11-02 under issue #2, after every project this briefing covers was deepened to source level. The snapshot above is unaltered; this addendum records what verification did to its claims.*
### What held

- **The frontier signal itself.** The briefing's only substantive claim — "prefer scoped, reversible self-improvement with regression gates" — held, and the deepening found it implemented almost literally, three times over. **KARMA** retains a candidate patch if and only if validation gain g_t ≥ θ = 0.01, otherwise restoring the pre-edit snapshot of its six-module knowledge directory; across 18 domain–model settings that gate produced an average hold-out gain of +8.96 points (30.19 → 39.15), improving 16 of 18 settings. **EvoTeam** decides a provisional team's admission to its team library only after execution, and prunes interaction rules that no longer fit. **MoLRSI**'s improvement loop is likewise disciplined by held-out evaluation: test outcomes were never used to guide search, and its splits are similarity-based rather than random. A one-line preference in a summary turned out to be the operating principle of the day's three self-improvement systems.

- **The discovery list itself.** All nine listed works were located and read in full at source level, and none was misidentified: nine real papers or product sources, each now carrying a deepened page in this knowledge base. Beyond listing them, the briefing made no per-project claims, so there are no per-project verdicts to record in this section; the qualifications below are what the listing's flat presentation left out — and they are substantial, because a uniform "Discoveries" heading presented nine very different evidence tiers as if they were one.

### What was corrected or qualified

- **All nine items.** Every result behind every listing is author-reported from a single source; no independent replication exists for any of them. The index's uniform presentation did not distinguish a randomized three-centre human study (ClinGeX) from a vendor changelog with no inspectable evidence at all (Copilot Fusion), nor a paper whose own audits undercut its intervention (Prefix Executability) from one whose headline survives its own ablations (KARMA). Read today, the list is a table of contents, not a ranking: the deepened pages, not the briefing, are where each item's evidence tier is stated.

- **GitHub Copilot Fusion.** Its claims remain vendor claims, unverifiable from public sources — this entry's status is different in kind from the other eight, and the expansion does not change it. The entire evidence base is GitHub's changelog post and product documentation: Fusion Max's Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index score of 60, "within two points of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 Max" at about half the cost and tokens and about 40% less time, is quoted by the vendor without methodology, task set, or variance, and there is no paper, artifact, harness, or per-task data behind any of it. GitHub's own sources do not even agree with each other — the changelog describes a single reviewing model in the Critique pattern, the documentation multiple parallel critics. Nothing here can be graded against a source, because the vendor's announcement is the only source there is.

- **EvoTeam.** Its figures were verified against v1 of the paper; arXiv lists a v2 revised 2026-10-30 against which the numbers have not been re-checked. What v1 supports is narrower than "evolving teams win": under per-benchmark evolution EvoTeam's average margin over Meta-Team is only +0.55 points (58.85 vs 58.30), and it loses LOCA-Bench outright (63.75 vs 69.58). The clean win is specific to the mixed-benchmark stream, where one system evolves over 116 shuffled instances and EvoTeam takes all six metrics (59.58 vs 55.55, +4.03). The verified claim is about where coordination knowledge should live — interaction rules team-scoped, individual skills global — not about general superiority, and several decisive margins sit within plausible run-to-run noise the paper never quantifies.

- **MoLRSI.** Likewise read at v1 with a v2 outstanding; and its two evidence tiers must travel together. The wet-lab results validate the end workflows: 17 hits among 107 tested compounds, a 15.9% hit rate against 8.7% (21 of 241) in the authors' previous expert-driven study — a cross-study comparison with no between-group hypothesis tests and a differently constructed comparator denominator — plus eight phage-display enrichment hits across two pMHC targets. The self-improvement claim itself rests on computational ablations only: pooled cross-task memory improved mean test AUPRC on all six antibacterial tasks, and on the held-out MART-1 target turned an enrichment factor of 0.00 into EF@1% = 3.65 and EF@2% = 5.55. The backbone, Claude Sonnet 5, is frozen throughout; what improves is a memory-conditioned search policy, and no code is released.

- **Pre-Crime for Agents.** The name promises more than the study delivers. Its narrow claims are well supported on its own data: a linear probe on the target's activations scores AUROC 0.98 [0.94, 1.00] per call at the moment of the leak on held-out Qwen3-32B episodes, catching 31 of 38 leaking calls at a 5.5% flag rate in refusing episodes. But episode-level forecasting — whether an agent will ever leak — is near chance (0.53 and 0.61 across the two models), the trained TF-IDF text classifier nearly matches the probe (0.96 against 0.98), and the probe reads coming disclosure, not rule violation: replayed under a rule permitting sharing, scores are unchanged (median change 0.00). It is a last-second tripwire, not the foresight the framing suggests.

- **EnvOS.** Its verification machinery is less independent than the listing implied: the reconstruction checkers are separate code paths by the same team — "independent" by the authors' own definition does not mean an independent group — and the external archive challenge was generated by a Gemini CLI agent rather than an independent team, and is precisely where the pipeline looks worst (25 of 39 designated corruptions caught; 12 of 39 counted clean). The paper also discloses an internal non-replication of the team's own earlier result. None of this erases the central measured effect — showing the current value at the decision point rescued 15/16 clinic episodes, against 2/16 for a reminder-plus-link and 0/16 for nothing, with only 1 of 16 cued trajectories ever following the link — but the assurance the framework offers is scoped to its own exercised checks, and its audits surfaced 11 real defects, 7 of which had already produced scored verdicts.

- **KARMA.** No replication is possible from public artifacts: neither code nor data is available — the data is proprietary AliExpress merchant logs — and every baseline (GEPA, AHE, Self-Harness) was re-implemented by the authors themselves, on KARMA's own interface and gate. Two further qualifications the flat listing concealed: the study is offline log replay in which no advertising action is ever executed, so "industrial" describes data provenance, not production evidence; and the regression gate does not guarantee regression-freedom — Kimi-K3 regressed on hold-out in two settings despite passing the gate (Automotive 40.31 → 37.19, −3.13; Electronics & Office 32.81 → 30.94, −1.88), each after a single early patch on a fixed 160-case validation set.

- **Prefix Executability.** Summary-level descriptions blurred this into an intervention; the contribution is a measurement protocol — a deterministic, judge-free replay scorer that asks whether every prefix of a tool-call trajectory was executable. Its durable finding is a divergence: under GPT-5.4 on BFCL, Plan-Conditioned ReAct and Dual-Loop tie on final success (0.94 each) while their trajectories are fully executable 47% and 94% of the time respectively; in the Llama-4 cell, 138 of 148 successful episodes (93.2%) succeeded only after an invalid prefix. The one intervention, Dual-Loop, is explicitly a probe, and its validation advantage failed to replicate in the paper's own τ³-bench transfer audit, with the paired sensitivity analysis returning a null — every paired interval includes zero. No code or rollout data is released.

- **ClinGeX.** Nothing in it is independently reproducible at present: the code is not public (only a registration-gated hosted demo), data are available only on request, and the agent-core selection scores exist only as radar plots, so the margin by which DeepSeek-V4 beat five alternative cores cannot be extracted or checked. The headline is nevertheless real as author-reported evidence — 71.7% macro-averaged concordance against 45.7% for the strongest comparator on 223 temporally held-out cases, scored by deterministic rules rather than an LLM judge — but the benchmark's cases are drawn from exactly the five resources the 105-skill library was purpose-built to encode, every evaluation was run by the authors' own institutions, and headline percentages rest on heterogeneous denominators (actionability concordance, for instance, uses 65 of 78 cases).

- **DynaRev.** Its contents were verified against the source text and its repository's existence confirmed — the three public data files (76 additive, 62 unconditional-override, 43 conditional-override tasks) sum to the paper's 181 — but the benchmark was not re-executed; verification here means reading, not reproduction. Reading surfaced what the listing could not: the benchmark's most actionable finding is narrow (rewriting the accumulated goal while preserving browser state raised Score from 0.647 to 0.718, on one model and an unspecified subset; Reset + Rewrite was worst at 0.525), the best strict task success across five models is only 43.1%, and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash serves as data constructor, milestone judge, and offline evaluator while also being the evaluated agent that posts that 43.1% — a circularity the paper does not measure.

### What the briefing could not yet see

- The frontier signal has a mechanism, and it recurs: in **KARMA**, **EvoTeam**, and **MoLRSI** the improvement lives outside the model — in gated, revisable external state — the pattern now named [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]]. KARMA edits only a knowledge directory with weights and code frozen; EvoTeam evolves team rosters, team-scoped rules, and global skills with parameters fixed; MoLRSI's frozen backbone improves only through its three memory stores. The briefing stated the preference; the deepened corpus shows the preference is a description of where capability actually accumulated in all three systems.

- The same day's work also shows who grades the improvement, and how often the grader sits inside the system — [[concepts/evaluator-circularity]]. **ClinGeX** is an instance: DeepSeek-V4 builds the skills, serves as the agent core, and generates the ablation items (scored 83.2% against 72.2% for the plain core on those self-generated items), while two further models judge; its strongest evidence, the rule-scored held-out benchmark, is precisely the part that escapes the loop. **DynaRev**'s constructor–judge–contestant is the same shape in benchmark form, and **MoLRSI**'s generation search optimized against its own ranking score. The escapes on this page are as instructive as the instances: KARMA's deterministic agreement scorer and Prefix Executability's judge-free replay leave no model grading its own work.

- A final-success score is a mixture, and the mixture ratio is a choice of instrument — [[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]]. Prefix Executability's decomposition makes it exact: the same BFCL condition reads as 0.74 final success or as trajectories only 5% fully executable. DynaRev shows the milder form — its five models rank one way by mean constraint Score (Gemini-3.8-Flash best at 0.735) and another by strict task success (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash best at 43.1%). A summary-level briefing necessarily reports one number per item; the deepened pages show the number is partly a property of the protocol that produced it.

- Several of the day's claims sit one tier below their framing — the gap named [[concepts/validation-tier-gap]]. MoLRSI's wet-lab tier validates workflows while its self-improvement tier is computational; KARMA's "industrial" is the provenance of logged data in an offline study that executes no action; Pre-Crime's title promises forecasting its own numbers reduce to last-second detection. In each case the underlying work is sound at its actual tier — the correction is to the altitude of the claim, not to the work.

- Verification's warrant is bounded by the checks actually exercised — [[concepts/scoped-assurance]]. EnvOS certifies environments against declared obligations and then audits its own grader, and still counted externally designated corruptions as clean; KARMA's gate guarantees improvement on a fixed validation set and demonstrably not on hold-out. The briefing's signal asked for regression gates; the deepened corpus adds the rider the summaries lacked: a gate is evidence about the set it ran on, and scoped assurance is the strongest kind any of these systems earns.

- Theme-level verdict, expanded: nine items listed flat concealed a single repeated question — where does an agent's improvement live, and who grades it — that became visible only once every item had been read at source level. The first half of the question is answered by the external-state pattern; the second half is answered, uncomfortably, by how many of the day's graders were built by the graded. The briefing could not see either half, because both are properties of the set, not of any item in it.

### Closing

Read today, this briefing is evidence of something narrower and more useful than its discoveries: it is a dated specimen of summary-level understanding — what nine pieces of work looked like, on 2026-11-01, before any of them had been read against its sources. Graded against those sources, its list was accurate and its one judgment was better than accurate: the frontier signal it extracted from summaries is the principle the deepened corpus found implemented, almost literally, in KARMA's gate and EvoTeam's admission rule. Its failures are the failures of the genre — flat presentation across evidence tiers, vendor claims beside randomized studies, framings one tier above their contents — and they are recorded above at full strength, in the addendum rather than in the snapshot, which stands as it was.
