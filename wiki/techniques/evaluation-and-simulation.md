# Evaluation and simulation

## What it is

This was the largest bucket in the KB's original summary-level grouping — eight candidate projects — and source-level mining gave it a split verdict, recorded on [[evaluations/relation-discovery-2026-11-02]]. The **evaluation** strand was confirmed as the corpus's richest vein, but inverted: the projects turned out to share not a technique they apply but the failure modes of applying it, and the strand's yield was the register's strongest concept ([[concepts/evaluator-circularity]], twelve pages) plus a family of measurement concepts. The **simulation** strand dissolved: no concept about simulation as such found two-sided evidence. This page honours that split rather than smoothing it over. It synthesises the evaluation craft the eight projects actually practise — benchmark construction, trajectory and outcome scoring, adversarial testing, and auditing the graders themselves — and then reads that craft through the five concept lenses that discount its numbers. Simulation is treated separately, in its own subsection below: in this corpus it is the venue evaluation happens in, not a method any project advances, and the discipline it needs is tier discipline, not technique.

The eight projects divide by where they intervene in the measurement pipeline. [[projects/codespecweave]], [[projects/angler]], and [[projects/webanything]] build the test material — tasks, attack cases, whole environments. [[projects/seq-k]] and [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] define scoring protocols over behaviour. [[projects/vericua]] verifies outcomes after execution. [[projects/envos]] certifies environments and audits verdicts. [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]] evaluates a monitoring instrument — a study of whether an overseer's detector works, which makes it evaluation pointed at the evaluation layer itself.

## Mechanism variants

**Executable verification over judgment.** The corpus's most repeated design move is replacing text matching and LLM judging with checks against state. REEF, [[projects/angler]]'s benchmark, reads success "directly from recorded website states rather than relying on agent self-reports or LLM-as-a-judge scoring," with three programmatic verifiers separating never-successful, transiently successful (recovered), and successful-at-termination attacks. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] scores generated contracts by exact transaction replay against the original contract — 60 sequences of 8 transactions, unverified steps scored as incorrect. [[projects/vericua]] synthesises executable *checkers* (read-only getter calls plus a predicate) and arbitrates typed evidence deterministically. [[projects/codespecweave]]'s tasks are valid only if executable test suites say so. The shared bet: a verdict anchored in state survives scrutiny that a judge's impression does not.

**Sequential and trajectory protocols.** [[projects/seq-k]] makes the attempt structure itself the variable: under Seq@K each attempt is conditioned on the history of earlier attempts and their feedback, so the metric measures revision capability rather than one-shot ability plus sampling luck. Its feedback modes form an information ladder — binary verdict, raw verifier diagnostics, LLM critique with or without rubric access — and the ladder is part of the protocol's definition, not a detail. [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]] labels calls by distance from the act (this call leaks / a leak within a round / the episode ever leaks), turning one detector into three different evaluations with three different answers. [[projects/vericua]] scores a completed trajectory from three views of state — the recorded trajectory, the current surface, and persisted state — because any one view can be gamed or can omit the decisive effect.

**Construction pipelines with quality gates.** Generating test material is itself an evaluation problem, and three projects build gates into the supply line. [[projects/codespecweave]] drafts task, specifications, and tests jointly, then runs multi-model solver trials and trajectory-level review to find tests that pass wrong patches or reject right ones, repairing and revalidating before acceptance. [[projects/webanything]] freezes each reconstructed site — bound to a digest — for examination by a verifier in a separate context that cannot edit the candidate, and routes each discrepancy by evidence sufficiency: local repair where existing evidence establishes the expected outcome, targeted recapture where it does not. [[projects/envos]] runs a three-stage lifecycle — construct, certify, execute — in which certification (intervention validity, observation consistency, feasibility, behavioural discrimination, scorer robustness) happens *before any agent runs*, on the frozen instances rather than the templates.

**Adversarial generation as evaluation.** [[projects/angler]]'s ANGLER treats the attack set as a search problem: an LLM mutates payloads inside a compiler-validated feasible space, a contextual bandit selects among candidates using verifier feedback from prior rollouts, and near-miss seeds — attacks that achieved their effect transiently but not at termination — are prioritised because they locate the remaining bottleneck. The measurement claim is that a fixed attack suite systematically underestimates vulnerability, because the suite cannot adapt to what earlier rollouts revealed.

**Auditing the instruments.** The corpus's most distinctive variant evaluates the evaluators. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] plants one-rule mistakes in real contracts, each with an untouched twin, and counts a catch only under strict side-picking — a protocol any proposed judge, analyser, or tester can be subjected to. [[projects/vericua]] submitted its disagreements with official gold labels to an adjudicator from a different model family. [[projects/envos]] challenges its own grader with sealed, rule-only, and externally generated archive sets, and emits every published number from a claim ledger that re-derives it from hashed artifacts. [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]] designs its metric — AUROC computed within scenario-and-round groups — so that scenario- or round-based shortcuts score exactly 0.5 by construction, and compares its probe against zero-shot monitors, heuristics, and a *trained* text classifier on identical calls.

**Simulation as substrate, not technique.** Four projects evaluate inside simulated venues: [[projects/webanything]]'s reconstructed, resettable local mirrors of real websites; [[projects/angler]]'s nine finite-state-machine-generated site clones; [[projects/envos]]'s synthetic workplace testbed with containerised services; [[projects/vericua]]'s headless emulator for its online arm. One further use is easy to miss: [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] simulates a *stakeholder* — its elicitation questions are answered by a model reading the original contract — so simulation sits inside the measurement loop, not just under it. No project in the set proposes a simulation methodology: no fidelity-versus-cost theory, no sim-to-real transfer measurement, no comparison of simulation against live evaluation as alternatives. Simulation here is infrastructure the evaluation consumes, which is exactly why the mining pass found no concept in it.

## Evidence and measured effects

Everything below is **author-reported** from single-team preprints; the pattern across them is what this page asserts.

**Self-checks and un-audited instruments fail at rates that change conclusions.** Of 219 [[projects/webanything]] candidates that passed the builder's self-checks, independent verification passed only 25.57% and failed 63.47%. [[projects/codespecweave]]'s review found alignment flaws in 73.9% of 3,067 pilot candidate tasks — tests that would have certified wrong patches or condemned right ones — and one construction batch attrited from 14,905 initial tasks to 766 verified ones. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]]'s planted-mistake audit is harsher still: the static analyser Slither caught 1.5% of guard-removal mutants; an LLM judge caught 65.0% of its mutants but flagged 80% of *untouched* contracts; a black-box LLM tester caught 17.5% at a 40% false-alarm rate. A catch rate quoted without its false-alarm rate, the study shows, is half a measurement.

**The protocol choice is worth points.** [[projects/seq-k]] found that gains from independent resampling and gains from feedback-conditioned revision correlate at Spearman −0.08 — the two protocols measure different capabilities — and that calibrating model selection with Pass@5 picked actors reaching 70.8% and 90.2% held-out Seq@5 where Seq@3 calibration picked actors reaching 96.8% and 98.4%. Within its feedback ladder, rubric-aware critics repaired 73.8% of failed criteria against 34.4% for binary feedback and 36.5% for no-feedback retries: the feedback convention, not the attempt budget, carried the effect. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] shows the scoring-rule version of the same phenomenon: the same 500 output sets yield a 19-point gap under a shared-interface metric, 35 under a fixed required interface, 34 under exact replay.

**Adaptivity raises measured vulnerability; engagement metrics overstate it.** ANGLER's adaptive search reached 20.36% average attack success against 15.24% for random selection from the same validated candidate pool, and discovered 30.8% more successful attacks at a fixed 50-rollout budget — the gap widening as verifier feedback accumulated. But its intermediate metric lies in the flattering direction: pop-up injection engaged agents at a 52.4% click-through rate yet converted to only 17.1% final-state success, and the two backbones that almost never engaged with injected page content were still compromised by direct injection at field-comparable rates. Final-state verification, not engagement, is the measurement that survives.

**Rigorous verification works, and its price is known.** [[projects/vericua]] led every compared judge on accuracy and false-positive rate across its online and offline settings while abstaining on 18–30% of episodes — and costs roughly an order of magnitude more calls than a single-call judge (150K–378K tokens per trajectory against 33K–61K for three judges combined). [[projects/envos]]'s audits did concrete work: certification, re-derivation, and the archive challenges surfaced 11 defects, 7 of which had already produced scored verdicts under defective code. And [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]] supplies the calibration every instrument claim needs: its activation probe scores AUROC 0.98 per leaking call, but a trained bag-of-words text classifier on the same calls scores 0.96 — most of the fancy instrument's lead over a zero-shot monitor comes from *training on labelled calls*, not from activations. Evaluating the evaluator changed what the result was evidence *for*.

## Failure modes and bounds

The concept layer names five discounts, and every project in this bucket pays at least one.

**[[concepts/evaluator-circularity]] — the contestant inside the grading path.** [[projects/webanything]]'s builder and verifier are same-family models ("their independence is contextual and procedural, not model-diverse"), and the same model judges its downstream benchmark. [[projects/codespecweave]]'s training set and benchmark come from one pipeline with one vendor's models as construction agents, solvers, and trained backbones, so part of its on-benchmark gain may be train/test distribution match. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] is self-referential at study level — baseline, prior benchmark, and proposed pipeline are one team's systems — and its cleanest anchor is therefore the gap over plain one-shot generation, not over its own predecessor. [[projects/seq-k]] is the concept's boundary case: frontier-LLM graders from the same families as the actors and critics — family overlap only, recorded to mark where the term stops explaining anything. The bound from inside the evidence: a single circular grader applied to all contenders preserves within-study fairness while shifting every absolute level by an unmeasured amount, so circular numbers are weakest exactly where most quoted — as absolute quality.

**[[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]] — the standard is an artifact too.** [[projects/vericua]]'s adjudicator confirmed 51 of 81 flagged verdict–gold contradictions: where a strong checker disagrees with official gold, gold was the wrong side roughly two times in three. [[projects/codespecweave]]'s 73.9% is fallibility measured at construction scale, before filtering. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] scores against a corpus that is itself noisy — 35.1% of its contracts contain placeholder markers — and counts mistakes no transaction sequence exposes, the best oracle's rate over *all* planted mistakes is 65.2%, not the 87.5% headline over exposed ones. The bound: fallibility is correctable in one direction — adjudication resolved most flagged contradictions — so noisy gold degrades precision rather than voiding measurement; but any score difference smaller than the standard's own error is not interpretable as a system difference.

**[[concepts/protocol-relative-scores]] — the number is a joint property.** Beyond the seq-k and intent-loss cases above: [[projects/webanything]]'s widest fidelity gap sits on a metric whose source side was replayed with JavaScript disabled while the mirror side was captured with it enabled, and its format-relaxed rescoring (Retrieve passes rising from 12.00% to 32.31%) was applied only to the fine-tuned model's outputs, with no relaxed baseline — a convention change that cannot support a comparison. [[projects/angler]]'s click-through rate is the same dial in metric-choice form. The concept's own internal bound matters: in intent-loss, rankings were stable across every scoring rule tried while magnitudes moved by 16 points — magnitudes and pass rates are always protocol-relative; rankings are protocol-relative when the convention changes what is attempted or which outcome dimension headlines, not, on this evidence, when identical outputs are merely re-scored.

**[[concepts/comparator-construction-shapes-margins]] — the opponent was built by one side.** [[projects/angler]]'s sole experimental comparator "selects one of the remaining candidates uniformly at random," a deliberately weak search policy: the +5.12-point headline measures adaptivity against random selection, not against the best alternative attacker, and competing adaptive systems appear only in a feature matrix. [[projects/vericua]]'s baselines are the authors' re-implementations, two of them reduced — one judge's four-stage pipeline collapsed into a single call, another's cut from six milestones to four — and restoring a baseline's budget measurably raised its score. [[projects/codespecweave]] is the benchmark-provenance mode: the margin is partly over a yardstick from the method's own supply chain. No page in the set re-runs an original system in original form beside its re-implementation, so the construction share of any margin is nowhere quantified.

**[[concepts/validation-tier-gap]] — framing above the evidence tier, and the simulation discipline.** This is where the dissolved simulation strand exacts its price. [[projects/angler]]'s nine "websites" are synthetic clones, not the real services they are named for, and transfer of the measured rates to real sites or other scaffolds is untested. [[projects/webanything]]'s headline count — 244 artifacts — is an inventory count: quality evidence covers roughly ten sites, and 131 of the 244 received no review beyond an HTTP 200 and a homepage screenshot. [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]] claims forecasting in its framing while its episode-level prediction sits near chance (AUROC 0.53 and 0.61); the supported claim is last-second detection. [[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]]'s elicitation gains are, by the authors' own statement, optimistic estimates, because the simulated client answers by reading the very contract fidelity is scored against. The tier discipline follows: a score earned in a clone, an emulator, a mock, or against a simulated stakeholder is evidence *at that tier*; it does not transfer upward by naming, and a framing that invites the higher tier without populating it has a gap whether or not any number is wrong. [[projects/envos]] is the corpus's counter-example — it refuses to pool its results into a model ranking and scopes its claims to what its conditions actually manipulated.

**The global bound.** Every result synthesised here is author-reported from a single-team preprint, unreplicated, and several of the strongest effects rest on small samples (ten to sixteen paired instances in [[projects/envos]]'s prospective blocks; 100 tasks in [[projects/codespecweave]]'s benchmark; 26–38 leaking calls behind [[projects/pre-crime-for-agents]]'s test headlines). The evaluation craft is real; its numbers should be carried at the weight their own pages assign them.

## Open questions

- The size of the circularity bias is unmeasured everywhere: no page re-grades the same outputs with a fully external grader. What fraction of the reported gains survives external re-grading?
- Is there a mandatory convention checklist — scoring rule, feedback mode, denominators, interface and observation coverage, comparator construction — below which a headline number should be treated as unquotable? The pages agree conventions must be reported; none proposes the list.
- Simulation-to-real transfer is asserted nowhere and tested nowhere in this set. What would a transfer measurement for a reconstructed environment or a cloned site even look like — matched tasks on the real service, at what cost and with whose permission?
- [[projects/envos]] proposes certified non-stationary environments as training grounds; whether training in certified simulated change improves agents is explicitly untested. The evaluation infrastructure's payoff claim is itself an unevaluated claim.
- Verification quality scales with cost ([[projects/vericua]] at roughly 10× a judge's tokens; [[projects/envos]]'s full apparatus). Where is the knee — which of these gates buys the most error detection per unit cost when a pipeline can afford only one?

## Evidence status

This page is a synthesis over the KB's own deepened pages; it introduces no external source and no new measurement. Every underlying result is **author-reported** from a single preprint per project, with no independent replication in the set, and each project page records its own evidence status and limitations, which this page's bounds section draws on. The five concept lenses are the KB's analytical constructs from mining passes 1–2, each checkable against its concept page. **Candidate-link verdicts:** all eight candidates are **kept** — each instantiates the evaluation strand substantively (construction: codespecweave, angler, webanything; protocol: seq-k, intent-loss; post-hoc verification: vericua; certification and audit: envos; instrument evaluation: pre-crime-for-agents). The **simulation association is demoted for all eight**: none is kept *as* a simulation project — webanything, angler, envos, and vericua use simulated venues as substrate, intent-loss uses a simulated stakeholder inside its loop, and seq-k, codespecweave, and pre-crime-for-agents run on live attempts, real execution, or mocked tools without advancing any simulation claim. That demotion is the mining record's verdict — simulation found no two-sided evidence as a technique — retained here as a substrate discussion under tier discipline rather than as a strand of the technique.
