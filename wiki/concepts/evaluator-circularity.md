# Evaluator circularity

## What it is

Evaluator circularity is the condition in which an evaluation is partly
produced by the thing being evaluated, so the reported score partly
measures self-agreement rather than external quality. It takes three
main forms in this corpus. In the first, the same model grades its own
outputs: the system that does the work also sits in the grading path.
In the second, the benchmark is built from the system's own substrate —
the test material shares construction machinery, data, or a knowledge
graph with the system under test. In the third, the acceptance criteria
are set and scored by the agent itself, so the contestant also authors
the yardstick. The forms stack: the strongest instances below combine
two or three at once.

Independence, by contrast, comes in grades — a separate context, a
separate code path, a different model, a genuinely external party — and
several pages use the word "independent" for the weakest of these.
Circularity is the failure at the other end of that scale: not a
verifier that is merely imperfect, but one whose verdicts are partly
the contestant's own output returning as judgment.

The corruption falls unevenly. [[projects/filingloop]] states the bound
on itself: "Using one grader for all configurations controls
cross-configuration fairness; it does not control this bias, and its
size is unknown." A single circular grader applied to every contender
keeps within-study comparisons controlled — everyone faces the same
judge — while the absolute level of every score is shifted by an
amount of self-agreement nobody in this corpus has measured. Circular
numbers are therefore weakest exactly where they are most often quoted:
as absolute levels of quality.

## Evidence for

**The contestant is the grader.** [[projects/filingloop]] is the
limiting case: "gpt-6.1-sol is simultaneously the FilingLoop backbone,
the grader, the bounded fact service's matcher, and the skill editor" —
and its reference labels are model-annotated besides.
[[projects/clingex]] runs the same pattern in a clinical system:
"DeepSeek-V4 is simultaneously the skill builder, the deployed agent
core, and the generator of the ablation items"; the ablation items were
generated by that model from source records and the responses scored by
LLM judges, the evaluated core's own family generating and judging its
test. [[projects/dynarev]] puts one model on both sides of a benchmark:
"DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash is used for data construction, milestone judging,
and offline evaluation" — and that model "is the one that posts the
highest strict success rate (43.1%)." [[projects/evoteam]]'s
circularity sits one layer down: "GPT-5.6-Luna performs task execution
and all internal judgments — team selection, composition, chairmanship,
and all three reflectors — for every method."
[[projects/hera]] shows the reference-standard variant: the grading
reference is the home laboratory's own sealed conclusions, so
"agreement partly measures how well the agent reproduces that lab's
conventions."

**The benchmark is the system's own substrate.**
[[projects/agenticds]]'s benchmark shares its foundation with the
system it tests: "benchmark construction shares a graph with the
retrieval engine" — the search system is examined over the same
knowledge graph it searches.
[[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] is
self-referential at the level of the whole study: "the baseline
pipeline, the benchmark that previously evaluated it, and the proposed
elicitation-first pipeline are all the same author team's systems," and
the baseline pipeline itself "grades itself with an LLM rubric judge"
(r = 0.15 against human raters).

**The criteria and the scorer are the system's own.**
[[projects/auctopus]] ran on "self-set, self-graded criteria." Its
criteria were precommitted before results were judged, but
precommitment fixes the timing of the yardstick, not its authorship.
[[projects/molrsi]] shows the optimization-side form: "the search was
tuned against the system's own scorer, so score inflation without
binding gains would be invisible at this stage" — improvement on a
home metric is not, without an external check, evidence of improvement
on anything else.

**Independence in name only.** [[projects/webanything]]'s "Builder and
Verifier are same-family models" — "their independence is contextual
and procedural, not model-diverse; correlated blind spots are possible.
The same model also serves as the Odysseys judge."
[[projects/envos]]'s "independent" reconstruction turns out to mean
that "independent refers to separate code paths developed by the same
team, not an independent research group or independent source data."

**The boundary case.** [[projects/seq-k]] is the weakest instance in
the entry: "Rubric benchmarks are graded by frontier LLMs (Sonnet 4.6,
GPT-5.4) from the same model families as actors and critics in the
pool." Family overlap only — no shared identity, no shared substrate,
no self-set criteria. It is recorded because it marks where the concept
stops: if family resemblance alone counted as circularity, almost every
LLM-judged benchmark would qualify and the term would explain nothing.

## Evidence against and bounds

The concept is falsifiable, and this corpus supplies its own bounds.

**A non-model scorer escapes it entirely.** KARMA — outside the
supporting set — bounds the entry from outside: "the gate's scorer is
the deterministic agreement metric, so there is no LLM-judge circularity
in scoring." KARMA's residual problem is selection bias instead.
Circularity is a property of model-in-the-loop grading, not of
self-improvement loops as such; a system can evaluate itself against a
deterministic standard without the phenomenon appearing at all.

**The circularity can be confined to a layer that does not produce the
headline numbers.** [[projects/evoteam]]'s self-judgment governs its
internal evolution, but final scoring "uses the benchmarks' native
evaluators… not the authors' own LLM judging final answers." Whatever
bias the internal loop carries, the reported scores are not self-graded.

**Circularity does not mechanically crown its own model.**
[[projects/dynarev]]'s judge-inside-the-benchmark "ranks only second on
mean Score, behind Gemini." The same model does post the highest strict
success rate, so the record is mixed — consistent with bias on one
metric and none detectable on the other. What the bound establishes is
that circularity biases rather than determines; this corpus cannot
separate the two readings, which is itself part of the problem.

**Procedural separation buys real signal.**
[[projects/webanything]]'s verifier is the same family as its builder,
yet the procedurally separated verifier still caught most of what
self-checks missed. Separation is a gradient — context and procedure,
then model diversity, then external independence — and even the weakest
step catches real errors. Circularity is a matter of degree, and the
first remedy is cheap.

**The demonstrated remedy is external audit.**
[[projects/intent-loss-in-smart-contract-synthesis]] is the one setup
in the set where a genuinely external check ran, and the flaw in the
authors' previous scorer surfaced via "four counterexamples an external
audit found in the authors' previous scorer." No internal arrangement in
this corpus has a comparable catch to its name.

**A designed mitigation can fail its own check.**
[[projects/hera]] built mitigation in — sealing, and a blinded second
grader — and the check partly failed: the second expert reached only
"65% (quadratic-weighted Cohen's κ = 0.38)" agreement, and "a pre-sealed
prediction of at least 0.70 exact agreement was not met." The attempt
was precommitted and the failure reported, which is the corpus behaving
well; but it shows that declaring a mitigation is not the same as the
mitigation working.

## Open questions

- The size of the bias is unknown in every instance. No page in this
  corpus re-grades the same outputs with a fully external grader to
  isolate the self-agreement component. What fraction of the reported
  gains survives external re-grading?
- Procedural separation demonstrably buys signal, but no page here
  compares a same-family verifier against a cross-family verifier on
  identical artifacts. What, if anything, does model diversity add?
- [[projects/dynarev]]'s split verdict — first on strict success,
  second on mean Score — is either the signature of circular bias or a
  coincidence. What measurement would tell the two apart?
- KARMA trades circularity for selection bias by using a deterministic
  scorer. Is that trade general, and which of the two failures is
  cheaper to audit?
- [[projects/seq-k]]'s family overlap marks the concept's outer edge,
  but the corpus offers no principled threshold: at what degree of
  overlap does a judge stop being independent?
