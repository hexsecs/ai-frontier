# HERA

## What it is

HERA (Hierarchical Experimentation and Reasoning Architecture) is an LLM agent that autonomously runs cryogenic transport measurement campaigns on quantum devices made from atomically thin materials — principally multilayer graphene, plus WSe2-proximitized bilayer graphene in a transfer study. It is built on the Claude Code harness, augmented with tools that drive laboratory instruments, by Zachary Gomez, Aaron Sharpe, and colleagues at the Stanford Institute for Materials and Energy Sciences (SIMES) and SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory (arXiv:2610.39074, October 2026).

What distinguishes HERA from a generic agent-with-instruments is its **corpus**: a lab-curated, version-controlled knowledge base of measurement protocols, software tools, worked examples, forward models, and literature, with explicit rules of evidence governing how conclusions may be stated. The corpus is the paper's central experimental variable. The study is as much an *assessment* as a demonstration: a blinded prospective trial against a sealed expert conclusion, a corpus-by-model ablation, 300 offline design replays across six models, and a domain-transfer test to superconductivity in a new material system on a second rig.

## Mechanism

**Session inputs.** A session begins from three operator-supplied documents plus standing instructions in the prompt: a *campaign brief* (goals, boundaries, allowed and banned operations, operations needing consent, potential starting points), a *device card* (machine-readable datasheet: identifier, composition, geometry, wiring, gate safety limits), and a *rig card* (instrument loadout, operating limits, quirks). Everything after that — which recipes to consult, what data to acquire — is the agent's choice. The agent must answer the brief's questions at a fixed set of confidence levels.

**The corpus.** At the freeze for the trial, it held 29 recipes totalling about 45,000 words (five rig variants), 24 worked examples covering 10 recipes, 13 papers converted to text with figures, two forward models (computing Fermi surfaces, Shubnikov–de Haas frequencies, and cyclotron masses for the candidate stackings of trilayer and tetralayer graphene), and a Raman feature-extraction model. Recipes outline a protocol — when to use it, what it measures, heuristics — and point to examples, tools, and related recipes. Software tools convert raw data to physical quantities (for example, gate voltages to carrier density and displacement field); their defined inputs and outputs force the agent to supply validated inputs, a design the paper calls a "pit of success" where the easiest path is the correct path. The stated design principle: "the corpus provides resources rather than instructions to execute."

**Rules of evidence.** A corpus style guide requires each rule to state the conditions under which it holds, each load-bearing claim to cite its source and say what kind of source it is, and every conclusion to carry a confidence level from a fixed list of five: **decisive, strong, weak, consistent-with, and uninformative**. The guide bars "supports", "suggests", and "indicates" from carrying the weight of a claim. Individual recipes tie specific observations to specific levels.

**Execution.** The agent works by writing code and commentary into two live Jupyter notebooks: a *measurement notebook*, where each measurement is designed and code drives the rig through QCoDeS instrument drivers and the lab's MeasureMe sweep framework, and an *analysis notebook* for plots, analysis, and conclusions. Through the notebook channel, the agent may append cells to the measurement notebook but cannot edit or delete them; whether it may execute them depends on the autonomy mode. In the analysis notebook it may edit and execute freely. The harness saves a full transcript, and each session's outputs are assembled into a session archive.

**Autonomy and safety.** Two modes exist: E0 (human-in-the-loop — the agent writes measurement cells, the operator executes them) and E1 (autonomous — the agent executes the cells it writes); in both, the agent executes its own analysis cells. Autonomy is granted by the operator with a phrase the brief names, after setup and safety cells run. The agent then performs a read-only state check against a list in the brief (for example, magnet at zero field, safety limits matching the device card). The safety cell sets the device card's limits on the instrument parameters, so an out-of-range setting raises an error before any hardware moves; the agent never changes a safety limit itself, and E1 engages only if every item passes. If the agent has any concern for the safety of the device or the rig, it stops, notifies the operator, and returns to E0. An *intervention* is any operator message after autonomy is granted; all are logged.

**Self-improvement loop.** After each session, the operator launches a review agent that reads the session archive, gathers findings from agent, operator, and observer notes and the transcript, and checks the session against every entry of an append-only fix log (past revisions plus deferred findings, each stating the evidence that would reopen it). For each finding it recommends revising the corpus, deferring, recording only, or dropping, and drafts the edit for revisions. The operator accepts or rejects each; accepted revisions become git commits and fix-log entries. Corpus and agent prompt are both under version control, so any past state can be checked out and re-run.

**Models.** Live stacking sessions used Claude Fable 5 and Opus 5 at high effort in Claude Code; the replays used six models — Fable 5, Fable 5.1, Opus 5, Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, plus GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra in OpenAI's Codex CLI; the transfer sessions used Claude Opus 4.8.

## Evidence

All results here are **author-reported**, from a single laboratory and paper; no independent replication exists. The main study's task is determining the stacking order of multilayer graphene (trilayer ABA vs ABC; tetralayer ABAB vs ABCA vs ABCB) from a Landau fan — resistance versus magnetic field and carrier density — where each Fermi-surface orbit contributes one Shubnikov–de Haas frequency, with cyclotron mass from the temperature damping of the oscillation amplitude as a secondary discriminator.

### The blinded prospective trial

The trial protocol was written and committed to the corpus repository on 17 July 2026; the corpus, prompts, and templates were frozen under a git tag on 19 August 2026, before the first trial session. The expert (senior author Aaron Sharpe) measured each device first; his conclusion and confidence were sealed as SHA-256 hashes committed before each session and withheld from both HERA and its operator. Devices and cards were screened for tell-tale words ("benchmark", "ground truth", "replication"); the agent received no prior measurement, no Raman data, and no sign the session was part of a trial. The protocol required every session to be published. Both sessions ran Fable 5 with the full corpus in autonomous mode E1.

HERA matched the expert's sealed conclusion on **both devices, in stacking order and in confidence level**: device 1 (trilayer) → **ABA, strong**; device 2 (tetralayer) → **ABAB, strong**. The tetralayer session ran with **zero** pre-conclusion operator interventions; the trilayer session had **one**, concerning a safety concern HERA itself raised over gate leakage. (A second trilayer intervention came after the conclusion — the operator's request for a finer fan; it resolved the lower ABA frequency and left the conclusion unchanged.) The trilayer reasoning shows the rules of evidence doing work: the fan resolved a single frequency consistent with the higher ABA frequency; the lower ABA frequency would have completed at most one oscillation in the fan's field range, so HERA counted it unresolved rather than absent and turned to the second discriminator — a measured cyclotron mass of m* = 0.049 ± 0.002 m_e against corpus model values of 0.060 m_e (heavier ABA band) and 0.11 m_e (ABC) — and concluded ABA. At API list prices the sessions cost $265 (device 1; 16.2 active hours, 42.1 wall) and $196 (device 2; 12.9 active hours).

The caveat belongs in the same breath: the endpoint is **agreement with a single expert, on n = 2 devices, one session each, one model**. The paper states this itself — "the protocol reports this agreement rather than accuracy" — and the expert's identifications rest on Raman spectroscopy plus his own transport measurements. This is strong evidence HERA can reproduce one expert lab's conclusions under that lab's own standards of evidence; it is not a measurement of correctness against independent ground truth.

### The corpus ablation

To isolate the corpus's contribution, Fable 5 and Opus 5 each measured the tetralayer device with the full corpus and with an **ablated corpus** that removed the recipes, worked examples, forward models, papers, style guide, and method documents, keeping only the cards, the software tools with their reference, and the safety limits (references to removed material were edited out of the prompt, cards, and tool reference too). With the full corpus, both models agreed with the expert's stacking order, one (Opus 5) initially at lower confidence. With the ablated corpus, Opus 5 concluded **ABCA — flatly wrong** against the expert's ABAB, relying on a false premise about Bernal tetralayer behaviour near charge neutrality; Fable 5 reached the right order but on claims its measurements did not support. In the paper's summary: "In both conditions the models planned reasonable measurements, but with the ablated corpus both took insufficient data to substantiate their claims."

Claim-by-claim expert grading (151 claims, 561 checkable items across the trial and ablation sessions) locates the effect precisely. **Verdict grades were 0.40 and 0.40 for the ablated sessions versus 1.00 for the Fable 5 trial session and 1.00 for Opus 5 with the full corpus** (the remaining full-corpus session, a Fable 5 repeat, scored 0.43 because a known misreading — see Limitations — carried into its verdict, though its order was right). Inference grades ran 0.44–0.58 ablated versus 0.68–0.91 full, while *decision* grades — what to measure and how — were high in all five sessions (0.71–0.96). The corpus therefore improved **inference and verdict quality, not measurement selection**. Whole-session grades matched: both ablated sessions were graded C and F on answering the brief (F glossed as "not alone with the rig"), against two of the three full-corpus sessions answering the brief as-is and the third after re-analysis.

A blinded second expert re-graded a fixed subset (56 claims, 212 items): exact agreement was 65% (quadratic-weighted Cohen's κ = 0.38), and a pre-sealed prediction of at least 0.70 exact agreement was **not met**. The ordering survived — under the second expert's grades every full-corpus session still outscored both ablated sessions — though at the boundary the margin was only 0.002.

### The offline replay study

Because live sessions are expensive and few, the authors built an offline replay: each replay restarts a fresh agent from the archived state of a full-corpus session just after its two Shubnikov–de Haas spot checks, and asks it to *design* the Landau fan (density and field ranges plus sampling). The fan is never measured. Six models each produced up to 20 fans per corpus condition — 300 replays, of which 239 ended in a fan the deterministic grader could score (the other 61 mostly designed another spot check instead, almost all in the full-corpus condition, whose recipe emphasises spot checks first). The grader, developed on other sessions' fans and frozen before any production fan was graded, estimates **P(call)**: the probability that the fan as designed could discriminate the stacking orders. Four metrics were pre-registered on 29 September 2026; the primary statistic is mean P(call) gain at an 8-hour cutoff, where a fan planned longer than 8 hours scores zero.

At that cutoff, every model's fans scored higher on average with the full corpus, for a **pooled gain of +0.43 in mean P(call)** (permutation test, p = 0.0004 after Holm correction across the four metrics). Per-model gains ranged from +0.10 (Fable 5) and +0.18 (Opus 5.5) to +0.72 (Fable 5.1) and +0.81 (GPT-6 Sol), largest for the models with the worst ablated baselines. The full corpus also cut the **median planned acquisition time from 5.5 h to 4.4 h** and halved the fans planned over eight hours (29 to 15); pairing fans across conditions per model, the full-corpus fan both scored higher and took less time in 45% of pairs versus 5% for ablated. Scoring the no-fan replays as zero still raised pooled P(call) by 0.23–0.45. One honest exception: at a 12-hour cutoff Fable 5's gain was −0.02, because eleven of its twenty full-corpus fans were planned at 9.4–20.4 hours. (Per-model *absolute* P(call) means appear only in a figure in the paper and are not reported here.)

### Domain transfer

HERA was then carried, harness and corpus unchanged, to a different material system on a different rig: finding and characterising superconductivity in WSe2-proximitized Bernal bilayer graphene in a dilution refrigerator (~30 mK base) rather than the stacking work's variable-temperature insert (~1.2 K). The expert composed the three required documents — new device card, rig card, and a superconductivity recipe encoding his standard of evidence — in a few hours. Transfer was not frictionless: the second session surveyed too narrow a displacement-field range and concluded, wrongly, that the device does not superconduct; one operator message pointing out that the accessible gate range had not been exhausted was enough for HERA to sweep higher and find it. That failure produced the study's most consequential recipe correction — cover the whole accessible gate range before any negative conclusion — plus an amendment stating the expected location of the superconducting region. With those corrections, HERA found and characterised the superconductor autonomously, including the temperature dependence of the critical current: session 6 ran fully autonomously (E1, 0 interventions, 12.6 h) and concluded "SC (decisive)", as did session 3; session 4 concluded "SC (strong)"; session 5 hedged at "consistent-with" after the operator ended it early. Extracted critical currents (8–30 nA), fields (≈1.8–3 mT), and temperatures (≈100–300 mK) sat within about a factor of two of a reference session — itself HERA-run with the operator in the loop, not an independent human measurement.

## Limitations

**Stated or demonstrated in the paper.** The trial measures agreement, not accuracy, against one expert — the senior author, who also graded the claims of record. Live samples are tiny: n = 2 trial devices, one session per ablation cell (two for Fable 5/full), and the second-expert check shows grading itself is only moderately reliable (65% exact agreement, κ = 0.38, sealed prediction of ≥0.70 failed). A corpus-gap failure mode is demonstrated, not hypothetical: every tetralayer session initially misread a pair of charge-neutrality resistance peaks — a known Bernal feature no recipe covers — as two device regions; the rules of evidence kept the error out of most full-corpus conclusions but not all, and with the ablated corpus the same misreading produced a wrong exclusion. The system is stochastic in consequential ways: during development, three consecutive sessions ran the same brief on an unchanged system and their conclusions flipped between ABA and ABC. The replay measures design against previously acquired data — the fan is never run — and its grader metrics were chosen from about twenty candidates by how well they separated the two corpora on development fans, and tend to favour the corpus's own recommended uniform-in-1/B grid (the 21 ablated fans using such a grid scored as well as full-corpus fans). The most common expert criticism across all sessions: they should have taken higher-resolution data. Scope: one laboratory, one instrument class, graphene-family devices, one live harness family.

**Analyst-assessed.** *Vendor entanglement:* the work was funded by an Anthropic AI for Science Program award (declared by the authors as a competing interest, with Anthropic stated to have had no role in design, analysis, or publication decisions); HERA was developed with Claude models, all live sessions used Claude models in Claude Code, and the two GPT-6 replay models ran in a different harness (Codex CLI), so cross-model replay comparisons confound model with harness. *Circularity:* the sealed conclusions, the corpus recipes, the grading, and the transfer recipe all trace to the same lab and largely the same expert, so agreement partly measures how well the agent reproduces that lab's conventions — the paper's stated goal, but a bound on external validity. *Ablation confound:* ablated sessions differed from full sessions in the brief and output contract as well as the corpus (asked to derive their own plan; weak/moderate/strong confidence scale instead of the five-level scale), so strictly the study ablates corpus-plus-brief together. *Cost asymmetry:* full-corpus sessions cost roughly 1.5–2.7× more and about 3× the active hours of ablated ones ($146–$265, 12.9–14.4 h, versus $100–$119, 3.9–4.1 h); the gain is real but not free, and the paper draws no cost-effectiveness comparison.

## Competing approaches

The paper situates HERA among LLM-agent laboratory systems but performs **no quantitative head-to-head comparison with any other agent system**. Named prior art: Boiko et al.'s autonomous chemical research (the Coscientist line, *Nature* 2023); Bauer et al. (2026) on autonomous quantum transport measurements of 2D semiconductors — the closest same-domain work; Cao et al. on agents for self-driving laboratories in quantum computing; Qiu et al.'s AIMS; Shi et al.'s Qumus. The study's actual controlled comparison is internal: HERA with its full corpus versus HERA with tools only. Other implicit baselines are the expert's own campaign (the sealed reference) and Raman spectroscopy, the conventional route to stacking order, which the agent was denied.

## Implications

The result most likely to outlive the specific system is that a curated knowledge base is a *measurable* intervention in agent quality, separable from the model: holding models fixed, removing the corpus degraded verdicts to 0.40, produced a wrong physical conclusion, and cost a pooled 0.43 of design quality across six models from two vendors — while leaving measurement *choices* largely intact. The contribution sits in inference discipline: rules of evidence, fixed confidence vocabularies, worked examples tying observations to conclusion strength. The highest-leverage human work in such a deployment is therefore corpus authorship and review — exactly where HERA's design keeps humans, in the operator's accept/reject role over the loop. Methodologically, the study offers an assessment template stricter than most of the field: hash-sealed conclusions committed before sessions, a publish-every-session rule, frozen systems under version tags, pre-registered replay metrics with a frozen grader, and a blinded second grader whose unfavourable numbers are reported, not buried. The transfer study tempers the autonomy story usefully: the system crossed to a new material and rig on a few hours of expert authoring, but its one confident false negative was caught by a human, and the durable fix was a corpus rule — the loop working as designed, with the human still in it. This pattern — verification discipline carried in curated context rather than in the model — is the same one this knowledge base tracks under [[techniques/verification-and-evidence]].

## Reproducible experiment

The skeleton of one is fully specified by the paper's own design, and needs no cryostat. Take the archived state of a completed session at a natural decision point (the authors used the moment just after two Shubnikov–de Haas spot checks), restart fresh agents from it under two corpus conditions — full corpus versus tools-only ablation, with prompt, cards, and tool reference scrubbed of removed material — and have each design the next measurement artefact (here, the Landau fan: ranges and sampling). Grade the designs with a deterministic grader developed on other sessions' artefacts and **frozen before any production design is graded**, under metrics and a primary statistic (mean quality gain at a fixed time-budget cutoff, permutation test with Holm correction) registered before the replays run. The design transfers to any domain where a session can be archived mid-course and the next artefact scored without executing it. Per the paper's availability statements, the harness, the prompts and corpus in the state used for each session, the replay and metrics tooling, every session archive, the pre-registration documents, the expert adjudications, and the sealed documents are deposited in the Stanford Digital Repository and mirrored on GitHub (see Sources). Those deposits were **not separately inspected** for this page, and no part of the study was re-run; treat the replication path as specified-by-the-paper, not verified.
