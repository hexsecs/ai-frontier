# FA-Agent: Multimodal Seismic First-Arrival Picking

## What it is

FA-Agent is an agent framework for picking seismic first arrivals on difficult land data, reported in the arXiv preprint "FA-Agent: An extensible multimodal agent framework for first-arrival picking in mountainous seismic data" (arXiv:2611.06555, physics.geo-ph). Version 1 was submitted on 4 November 2026 and version 2 on 5 November 2026; the arXiv comment states "Submitted to GEOPHYSICS (under review)", 45 pages, 9 figures, 1 table and 3 supplementary movies — that is, a preprint under review with no peer-reviewed venue claimed. The source lists two authors, Wenda Li and Congcong Yuan; the acknowledgements name the Earth Observatory of Singapore and the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Nanyang Technological University, as supporters.

The problem framing is unusual in that the target is not prediction but a decision. First-arrival traveltimes constrain near-surface velocity models and static corrections, but in mountainous land data with a low signal-to-noise ratio the first arrival is weak, intermittent and masked by strong direct waves and noise. Energy-based pickers then pick later arrivals or noise, and deep-learning pickers need retraining for a new survey. The authors argue that because traveltime tomography combines many overlapping raypaths and least-squares inversion is sensitive to erratic data, an erroneous pick biases the velocity model while a missing pick removes only one of many overlapping constraints. Their stated conservative principle: for difficult zones, remove the pick rather than keep a doubtful one.

The agent is described by the authors as restricted to neither mountainous data nor any particular model, and structured "like a software package": a multimodal model that performs visual recognition and decides, tools that process the data deterministically, and skills that carry experience of processing mountainous data. The authors claim, as an author claim, that using a multimodal model's visual judgment to decide window by window whether first-arrival picks should be kept, corrected or removed "has not been examined" before.

## Mechanism

FA-Agent has three components. The **model** is the decision maker: it reads task instructions, looks at seismic images, decides what to examine next and calls tools. The **tools** are local programs performing deterministic operations — reading SEG-Y data, attenuating anomalous energy with a denoising module, computing the conventional picks, rendering whole-gather and enlarged views with or without picks, converting visual anchors into traveltimes, and exporting and checking results. All views use automatic gain control with a 400 ms window and maximum gain 30. The **skills** are written instructions with reference cases describing the workflow order, what to look for in the whole gather, how to examine a questionable zone, and when a pick should be kept, corrected or removed. Because judgement is made by the model and processing by the tools, each decision can be traced to the image it was based on; each recorded decision assigns an action, a reason and anchors to each trace segment.

**The workflow has three stages.** First, *conventional initial picking*: the initial pick combines an envelope energy ratio (Allen, 1978) with an AIC onset score (Maeda, 1985) under a lateral continuity constraint that links picks across the gather. The continuity constraint can pull the curve onto a strong later event, such as the more energetic direct wave, or carry it through zones where no first arrival is visible. The initial pick is explicitly not accepted; it is the reference the agent examines. Second, *visual recognition of attention windows*: the agent first views the whole gather **without** picks so that its reading of the first-arrival pattern is not biased by the drawn curve, identifies the earliest coherent wavefront, its branches and the noisy or missing traces, then overlays the initial pick and marks a few attention windows where the pick and the visible wavefront disagree or the wavefront is uncertain. Third, *visual correction and removal*: each window is examined in enlarged views, first without and then with the initial pick, with enough surrounding traces to show the local trend; the agent may compare the original with a denoised version, divide the window into segments and make one decision per segment. A pick is kept if it lies on the visible wavefront, corrected by placing anchors on the visible wavefront if it departs from it, or removed if the first arrival cannot be established. Short interruptions of a wavefront visible on both sides may be bridged, but picks are never extended into zones without visible signal. Removal leaves the pick blank while the trace and its initial pick are retained. Finally the agent reviews the assembled picks on the whole gather and on each window, resolving jumps at segment joints and picks that moved onto a later branch.

**Skill development is on field data, not in advance.** From 1,000 randomly sampled receiver-line gathers, part were selected as development cases. On those cases the agent carried out corrections in consultation with "a geophysicist with many years of experience in processing mountainous seismic data"; the expert marked problem zones and judged ambiguous events, and the corrections and judgements were summarised into skills, with no change to model weights. The resulting skills are organised into modules for the overall workflow, whole-gather recognition of attention windows, local inspection and correction of a window, and the decision standard for keeping, correcting or removing. The same mechanism continues in production: when an expert reviews a processed gather, the agent writes it into the case library of the skills as a new case.

**Model and deployment.** The visual review in this study was carried out interactively with the multimodal large language model GPT-6 Astra, following the skill format of that environment. No separate API requests were issued, so per-call model metadata are not available. The paper states FA-Agent is not tied to this model and that the workflow, tools and skills can be used with any model that can view images and call tools.

**Data and setup for the field evaluation.** The data come from a three-dimensional land survey in a mountainous area: 490 shots, each recorded by 28,800 channels on 40 receiver lines of 720 channels, for 14,112,000 traces, each trace 751 samples at a 4 ms sampling interval. The shot gather is the basic processing unit. The agent processed all 1,000 randomly sampled gathers and classified each by first-arrival difficulty from its display: class A (obscured, broken or strongly mixed over large parts), class B (locally weak or noisy) and class C (clear and continuous). Of the 1,000, **12 were class A, 395 class B and 593 class C**. Three gathers are presented — 0813 and 0831 (class A, poor data quality) and 0001 (class C) — all outside the case library.

## Evidence

All results below are **author-reported** from this single preprint; none has been independently replicated. Two metrics need definition before the numbers can be read.

*Picking accuracy* is the percentage of picks falling within ±4 ms, ±8 ms and ±20 ms of the true first arrival (one, two and five samples at the 4 ms interval). *Signal-to-noise ratio (S/N)* is the ratio of the root-mean-square amplitude of the signal just after the true first arrival to the RMS amplitude of the noise on the same trace.

**Semi-synthetic test 1 — strong noise, first arrival present.** The signal is a finite-difference synthetic shot gather with 400 receivers at 10 m spacing, source near trace 211, resampled to the 4 ms interval and 751 samples of the field data, with amplitude decreasing exponentially with offset. The true first arrival of each trace is defined on the noise-free gather as the first sample whose absolute amplitude exceeds 1% of the trace maximum. The noise is real field noise: the part of each field trace recorded well above the first arrival, extended to full length by mirroring in time, so that vertical noise bands, low-frequency interference and trace-to-trace noise-level variation are preserved. At the moderate level the median S/N is 60 near the source and about 2 beyond 150 traces; at the strong level the noise is doubled, so the far-offset median S/N falls to about 1. The agent did not know the truth or how the gathers were built, and the skills were not modified for the tests.

| Test 1 condition | Conventional pick | FA-Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Moderate noise | only 2% of picks within ±20 ms (curve flattens the apex into a plateau) | **all** picks within ±20 ms; about 60% within ±8 ms |
| Strong noise (doubled) | only 2% of picks within ±20 ms | all but one pick within ±20 ms; about 60% within ±8 ms |

The conventional pick follows the first-arrival wavelet near its energy maximum, about 40–50 ms after the true onset. Doubling the noise changed the agent's accuracy by only a few percentage points, which the authors attribute to the agent following the wavefront across noisy traces rather than relying on the amplitude of individual traces.

**Semi-synthetic test 2 — weak and hidden first arrivals.** Using the same synthetic gather, only the first-arrival wavelet — the 90 ms following the true onset — was weakened, and later events were left unchanged. Beyond 80 traces from the source the first-arrival amplitude decreases gradually to half its original value at the gather ends; it was also hidden in four blocks of traces by reduction to 4% of original amplitude, two wide (traces 60–92 and 335–370) and two short (traces 122–134 and 300–311). Noise came from a different class A field gather (0901) at the moderate level. The conventional pick's continuity constraint carried it onto the stronger later events over the whole gather, and **none** of its picks lies within ±20 ms of the truth. The agent recovered the visible first arrival on both flanks and across the apex, removed the picks in the two wide hidden blocks and bridged the two short ones. **All 324 picks the agent kept lie within ±20 ms of the truth, and 56% lie within ±8 ms.**

Per-trace decision quality, using a per-trace S/N classification of the first arrival (visible S/N ≥ 1.5, 271 traces; marginal 0.5–1.5, 39 traces; not visible < 0.5, 90 traces):

| Trace class | Traces | Agent behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Visible (S/N ≥ 1.5) | 271 | picked all |
| Marginal (S/N 0.5–1.5) | 39 | picked 92% |
| Not visible (S/N < 0.5) | 90 | removed 81%; the remaining 19% are inside the two short hidden blocks, bridged between visible wavefronts, and also within ±20 ms of truth |

None of the **76 removed traces** had a visible first arrival. The authors read this as the conservative principle being honoured: pick where the arrival can be seen, remove it where it cannot, and never replace a hidden first arrival with a later event.

**Field-data examples (author-reported decisions, no independent accuracy measure available).**

| Gather | Class | Kept | Corrected | Removed | Picks left | Removed share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0813 | A | 103 | 307 | 310 | 410 of 720 traces | 43% |
| 0831 | A | (not broken out) | (not broken out) | (not broken out) | — | 35% |
| 0001 | C | (not broken out) | (not broken out) | (not broken out) | — | 19% |

The paper states the per-gather keep/correct/remove counts only for gather 0813; for 0831 and 0001 it gives removal proportions, so those two rows carry no disaggregated counts.

Specific author-reported corrections: in 0813 the initial pick lay on a strong event about 110 ms later than a weak branch that could be followed from about trace 105, and the agent corrected traces 105–209 onto that branch, removing picks to its left where no earlier branch could be established; in window V2 it moved picks onto a weak ascending wavefront over traces 210–248 by up to 32 ms, and removed picks over traces 249–296 where no reliable wavefront existed on either side of a 48-trace gap; at the apex it followed local undulations with changes within 16 ms; it removed the far-offset picks beyond about trace 562 where vertical noise and low-frequency interference covered the expected first arrival. Seven short interruptions of a wavefront visible on both sides, 39 traces in total, were bridged. In 0831 the agent replaced a continuity-flattened plateau at 224 ms with the curved visible wavefront reaching about 176 ms, corrected traces 195–237 onto a weak oblique wavefront moving the picks earlier by 44 ms on average and up to 96 ms, and removed traces 141–194. In 0001 it moved the picks of traces 440–580 onto an earlier wavefront by 47 ms on average and up to 132 ms, and removed traces 581–615.

**Cost.** Correcting one gather took about **30–90 s on a single core of a local AMD CPU**, depending on gather difficulty. A commercial large language model additionally consumes tokens, mainly for image input, which adds cost when many gathers are processed. Per-call model metadata are not available because no separate API requests were issued, so no token total is reported.

## Limitations

**Stated by the authors.** Expert picks in such data are themselves subjective and cannot serve as an objective reference, which is why the semi-synthetic tests were run first. The field data are confidential and cannot be released. The complete code of the framework, tools and skills "will be made publicly available after the initial review of this paper", so at the time of writing no runnable artifact exists. A locally deployed multimodal model is proposed for confidentiality and per-call cost, but is future work. The paper presents three supplementary movies of the reviewed gathers as the record of the agent's decisions.

**Assessed from the reporting.**

- **No artifact yet, and confidential data.** The code is promised after review, the field data cannot be released, and no repository URL appears in the inspected text. Nobody can rerun the reported runs.
- **The field-data evidence is three hand-picked gathers with no objective reference.** The authors state this themselves. No accuracy figure exists for the 1,000 processed field gathers; the only objective accuracy numbers come from semi-synthetic gathers whose noise is real but whose signal is synthetic, and whose construction the agent did not know. Removal is 43% of traces in the hardest presented gather, so coverage is traded away — and no coverage-normalised comparison against an expert or a conventional picker on the field data is provided.
- **The skills were developed with expert supervision on the same survey family.** Development cases came from the same randomly sampled 1,000 gathers of the same survey, so the presented examples are held out from the case library but not from the survey's acquisition characteristics. Generalisation to another survey is asserted but not measured.
- **The metric is self-referential in the field setting.** "The visible wavefront" is defined by the model's visual judgement, and the decision standard is the skill text. Nothing in the pipeline gives a ground truth for the field data, so the paper's quantitative claims there are counts of decisions, not accuracy.
- **Single model, interactive use, no per-call accounting.** GPT-6 Astra was used interactively; no API requests were issued, so per-call model metadata, token counts and latency attribution are absent, and no comparison across models is run.
- **The conventional baseline is one picker.** The initial picker is a single energy-ratio + AIC + continuity-constraint algorithm; no deep-learning picker, and no alternative conventional picker, is used as a comparator. The paper frames FA-Agent as complementary to existing pickers and says other pickers can be added as tools, but that extension is not evaluated.
- **Cost is asserted, not measured per gather at scale.** 30–90 s of local CPU time is the tool-side cost; the image-token cost of the commercial model is left unquantified.
- **Related-work comparison is qualitative.** The claim that window-by-window visual triage is new is an author claim about prior art.

## Competing approaches

The source compares FA-Agent against the first-arrival picking literature and against prior LLM-agent work in geophysics.

**Conventional pickers.** Automatic pickers exploit short-term to long-term average ratios (Allen, 1978), AIC onset estimators (Maeda, 1985; Sleeman and van Eck, 1999), correlation and statistical criteria (Gelchinsky and Shtivelman, 1983) and waveform correlation (Molyneux and Schmitt, 1999). Other pickers use energy ratios (Coppens, 1985), fractal dimension and entropy (Boschetti et al., 1996; Sabbione and Velis, 2010), image segmentation of energy-ratio records (Mousa et al., 2011) and instantaneous traveltime (Saragiotis et al., 2013). Weak first arrivals can be enhanced before picking, by the τ-pp transform of energy-ratio records (Mousa and Al-Shuhail, 2012) or by interferometry (Place et al., 2019). Gather-level consistency constraints improve picks and reject erroneous arrivals (Sabbione and Velis, 2010), and global path tracing enforces stability and smoothness of the picked curve (Zhang et al., 2022). Interactive and assisted schemes reduce but do not remove manual effort (Giroux et al., 2009; AlTheyab and Schuster, 2016).

**Deep-learning pickers.** From convolutional waveform classification (Yuan et al., 2018) and combined convolutional-recurrent networks (Yuan et al., 2020) to U-net and pixel-wise segmentation (Hu et al., 2019; Ma et al., 2020), multistage segmentation (Wang et al., 2024) and three-dimensional U-net picking with waveform and energy-semblance inputs (Han et al., 2022); self-training across domains (Ozawa, 2024) and refinement of predicted arrival times (Pu et al., 2024). For mountainous data specifically, Li et al. (2024b) incorporated attention gates and energy-semblance information. Performance on a new survey depends on acquisition conditions, waveforms and noise, and fine-tuning and transfer learning reduce the mismatch but require adaptation data (Mardan et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a). Hybrid methods detect and correct mispicks with a neural network (Duan and Zhang, 2020), and a U-Net can correct first-arrival traveltimes from refraction interferometry (Lee and Pyun, 2024).

**LLM and agent work in geophysics.** The paper notes LLM agents that select and execute seismic processing tools from natural-language requests (Kanfar et al., 2025), automate seismic wave simulation (Ren et al., 2025), and conduct multistep seismological analyses with auditable evidence chains (Liu et al., 2026); Hennenfent et al. (2026) embed expert judgement in agents that diagnose and attenuate seismic noise. The authors' stated distinction is that these agents orchestrate processing, modelling or analysis workflows, whereas FA-Agent uses visual judgement to decide per window whether a pick should be kept, corrected or removed. That distinction is an author claim, not an independently established taxonomy.

## Implications

The following is analysis by the KB maintainers, not a claim of the authors.

The most transferable idea is the reframing of quality control as a decision problem with an explicit asymmetric cost. The paper's premise — that a wrong pick is more damaging than a missing one — is a general pattern in [[concepts/ground-truth-fallibility]] and [[concepts/abstention-as-first-class-verdict]]: in tomography an erroneous datum biases an inversion, while a missing datum removes one of many overlapping constraints. Once the cost asymmetry is stated, "remove the pick" becomes a legitimate output rather than a failure, and the 43% removal rate on the hardest gather stops looking like a defect and starts looking like a coverage-versus-precision decision that has to be made explicitly. Engineering teams adopting this pattern must decide the asymmetry first and instrument coverage alongside accuracy, because a system that abstains well can look mediocre on a coverage-blind scoreboard.

The second takeaway is architectural: the agent is a *triage* layer over an existing deterministic picker, not a replacement for it. The initial pick is treated as a hypothesis to be examined, and the expensive model call is spent only on the small fraction of the gather where the hypothesis and the visible wavefront disagree. That is the [[concepts/improvement-lives-in-external-state]] pattern applied to a domain where the "external state" is an image and a SEG-Y file rather than a tool result. Teams porting this idea should preserve the deterministic front end and confine the model to disagreement triage, because the semi-synthetic test shows the value comes entirely from correcting the initial pick onto the visible onset, not from picking better in the first place.

Third, the separation of expert knowledge into written skills with a growing case library is the mechanism by which the system improves without weight updates, and it is [[techniques/agent-skills-and-memory]] in a domain where the memory is genuinely domain-specific. Its weakness is equally transferable: the skills encode one expert's judgement on one survey's data, and the paper provides no way to test whether the skill text generalises to another survey, another expert, or another acquisition geometry. Any adoption should treat skill versioning and cross-survey review as the deliverable, not the picking accuracy.

Fourth, the cost structure is worth copying deliberately: the deterministic work is local and cheap, while the model cost is dominated by image input. A pipeline that renders many images per decision will pay for tokens whether or not the decisions are valuable, so the "look at the whole gather first, then enlarge only the doubtful windows" discipline is a cost-control mechanism as much as an accuracy mechanism.

For contrast within this knowledge base, the triage-with-abstention pattern of [[projects/st-bench]] and the broader agent-evaluation work of [[projects/auto-research]] both hinge on a comparable question of what counts as a successful outcome when the system is allowed to decline; FA-Agent's contribution is that the decline is expressed as a *blank pick* in a physical artifact rather than as an abstention label, which is why the [[concepts/verified-completion-vs-apparent-success]] distinction between a wrong answer and a missing answer has a direct operational counterpart in the tomography inversion. These are analyst-level comparisons only, not claims that the projects cite or depend on each other; this page also contributes to [[evaluations/briefing-synthesis-2026-11-03-to-09]] and its acceptance and preservation implications should be read against [[concepts/preservation-aware-agent-improvement]].

## Reproducible experiment

No artifact exists yet and the field data are confidential, so a faithful replication of the field results is impossible. A feasible independent check of the core claims, without the confidential data, is:

1. **Construct the semi-synthetic protocol from the paper's specification.** Build a finite-difference synthetic shot gather with 400 receivers at 10 m spacing, source near trace 211, resampled to 4 ms and 751 samples with exponentially decaying offset amplitude; define the true first arrival as the first sample exceeding 1% of the trace maximum; take real field noise from a trace segment above the first arrival, mirror it in time, and assign field traces to synthetic traces in order. Scale the noise to the paper's two levels (median S/N 60 near the source falling to about 2 beyond 150 traces, then doubled to about 1). Pass criterion: a conventional energy-ratio + AIC picker with a lateral continuity constraint should place **only about 2%** of its picks within ±20 ms of the truth, and the agent-corrected picks should land **essentially all** within ±20 ms with about 60% within ±8 ms. A materially different ratio means the paper's claimed gap between a conventional pick and a visually triaged pick does not reproduce on your data.
2. **Build the hidden-block variant.** Weaken only the 90 ms after the true onset, taper amplitude to half beyond 80 traces, and suppress four blocks to 4% (two wide, two short). Pass criterion: no conventional pick within ±20 ms; all agent-kept picks within ±20 ms with about 56% within ±8 ms; of the not-visible traces (S/N < 0.5) at least about 80% removed; and none of the removed traces carrying a visible first arrival. The removal-coverage criterion is the one to watch — a system that removes fewer than the paper's rate is trading accuracy for coverage, and the direction of that trade must be reported, not hidden.
3. **Measure the abstention trade explicitly.** Report, alongside accuracy, the fraction of traces left blank and the fraction of true arrivals left unpicked. This is the check the paper does not run, and it is the one that decides whether the method is usable in a tomography inversion: a method that removes 43% of traces changes the effective raypath density, and its benefit must be weighed against that.
4. **Blind expert comparison on real data.** Take 100 gathers with expert-consensus picks on ambiguous zones only, apply the keep/correct/remove triage, and score retained coverage, error conditional on retention, correction magnitude distribution and false confidence (picks kept on a later event). Freeze the model, display gain, skills and budget; pre-register the acceptance criteria and hold out an untouched set.

Substituting a different multimodal model is unavoidable, since GPT-6 Astra may not be available; the substitution and any difference in display gain should be reported with the results.
