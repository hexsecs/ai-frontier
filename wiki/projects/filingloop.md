# FilingLoop

## What it is

FilingLoop (Zhang, Zhao, Gan, Sun, and Cong; arXiv:2610.37905, October 2026) is two coupled artifacts, and the page treats the benchmark as the primary one. The first is an interactive benchmark of corporate disclosure-review tasks reconstructed from historical SEC comment-letter correspondence in the EDGAR archive, predominantly IPO registration reviews. The second is a self-evolving agent framework — a Reviewer in the regulatory-staff role and a Filer in the issuer-counsel role — whose written procedures are updated across cases from requirement-level grading feedback while model weights stay frozen.

Each benchmark task is one historical review round. The agents receive the reviewed filing and the permitted earlier history; the actual staff comment letter, the company's replies, and subsequent amendments are hidden and serve only as reference evidence. The hidden reference decomposes every historical staff comment into atomic requirements, each recording the requested action, a disposition type, the target deliverable, a supporting quotation, and any company facts the response required. Of the 6,099 requirement occurrences across the corpus, 76.5% (4,668) are disclosure changes that must appear in the final filing — a reply merely claiming a revision was made does not satisfy them — 17.6% (1,075) are explanations to staff, 3.0% (183) are commitments, and 2.8% (173) require supporting material; 50.5% need company facts. The full corpus holds 380 tasks from 96 companies in 98 review packages, with 3,012 scorable comments, review dates spanning May 2016 to March 2026, and no train/validation split — all 380 tasks form a single evaluation stream. The work is document-heavy: the median primary filing runs 111,804 words, the median task carries 4 scorable comments (90th percentile 21), and 62.7% of comments contain multiple requirements.

## Mechanism

**Interaction protocol.** The two roles share one backbone model per configuration but work in isolated workspaces with distinct instructions and information access. The Reviewer inspects the permitted documents and raises grounded concerns: each comment carries a stable ID, a substantive request, a source document, and a supporting quotation that must match a supplied document under controller source checks. The Filer answers each active comment with exactly one response — `revised`, `explained`, `information_requested`, or `declined` — and submits edits as separate find/replace transactions linked to comment IDs. A trusted host maintains the authoritative draft, apart from workspace copies: an edit is committed only if its target text matches uniquely (exact match first, then a unique whitespace-normalized match), and ambiguous or missing targets are rejected with reasons. The Reviewer then reassesses revised artifacts, marking each concern resolved, unresolved, or awaiting company information, and may raise new grounded concerns. The budget is at most 3 review–revision exchanges and 100 agent steps per role invocation, with up to 16 tasks running concurrently; interaction ends when no active work remains or the budget is exhausted, but scoring still counts every eligible requirement, including concerns the Reviewer never raised.

**Bounded company-fact service.** When the Filer responds `information_requested`, the host matches the stated concern against current-round reference comments and releases only evidence-supported facts tied to that concern. It never exposes reference IDs, the historical reply, or the full fact ledger; released facts are attributable company statements, not certified truth, and an empty answer means limited evidence coverage rather than that the company lacks the information. Facts enter only the Filer's private history — the Reviewer sees them only if the Filer puts them into a formal response or the filing.

**Independent grading.** A separate evaluator grades the frozen final artifacts with a single grading model (gpt-6.1-sol) in three stages: matching generated comments to historical concerns by substance and requested action (a grounded extra concern is not automatically an error), fulfillment checks that give every requirement of a matched comment a binary verdict with an artifact identifier, quotation, and reason, and an audit of extracted company assertions and final-document changes against permitted evidence, flagging unsupported claims, contradictions, and harmful revisions. Outputs are schema-constrained JSON; the controller validates identifier coverage and quotation provenance before scoring. The headline metric, Requirement Accuracy, is fulfilled-and-discovered requirements over *all* eligible requirement occurrences, with denominators fixed before execution — undiscovered concerns stay in the denominator. Comment Accuracy requires all of a comment's requirements fulfilled; Task and Company Accuracy require every requirement in a task, or across all of an issuer's rounds, to pass. Harm is a separate task-level flag for at least one harmful filing change and is not subtracted from accuracy. Role diagnostics are asymmetric by design: the Reviewer score is discovery over the full reference inventory, while the Filer score is conditional — both variants are scored post hoc on the target pool the Fixed run assigned (2,162 requirements from 983 comments), so Filer percentages describe fulfillment under the joint policy on a shared scope, not an isolated Filer-only effect.

**Procedural evolution.** Both arms of the experiment traverse the same seeded stream (company order seed 0, round-robin interleaving, chronological rounds within a company, batches of at most 16 tasks with no company repeated in a batch), with identical base prompts, model, tools, budgets, and empty learned skills at the start. Skills stay frozen until every task in the batch has been executed and scored in *both* arms, so completion order cannot leak guidance within a batch; only then are first-attempt scores locked and feedback released. A skill editor — also gpt-6.1-sol — receives the Evolving arm's locked batch feedback (discovery outcomes, applicable requirements, fulfillment decisions, evidence quotations, reasons) and, separately per role, returns either `no_change` or a complete replacement skill: a Markdown document of procedural instructions with YAML metadata, around a 1,500-token advisory length. The update rule is validate-or-keep: validation checks structure, YAML validity, and role identity, and rejects known company identifiers/CIKs, private source paths, and sufficiently long exact copies of reference text; the editor gets at most 3 attempts per role, and if no valid update emerges the incumbent skill is kept. Validation never selects updates by held-out performance. Updated guidance applies only to later, unattempted tasks, updates stop after the penultimate batch, and cryptographic policy hashes bind each task to the guidance frozen before it started. What transfers is procedure, not facts — for example, Filer guidance to project edits onto the draft and then search the whole filing for surviving contradictions. (One traced case of such a rule fixing a later inconsistency comes from an earlier 64-task run and, as the authors note, supplies no additional observation to the main comparison.)

**Reference annotation.** The benchmark's ground truth is itself model-assisted: initial annotation by gpt-6-astra, an independent audit by claude-opus-5.5 blind to the annotator's handling and answerability decisions, reconciliation and further audit, and quotation checks, with the project team verifying the original collection; the small annual-report extension was annotated and audited by gpt-6.1-sol. The paper states explicitly that model agreement is not treated as measured label accuracy.

## Evidence

All results are **author-reported**, from a single seeded evaluation stream on the authors' own benchmark, graded by the single model discussed under Limitations. Conditions common to every configuration: 380 tasks, order seed 0, at most 3 exchanges, 100 steps per role invocation, default reasoning effort "high," one first-pass attempt per task, and the same backbone serving both roles within a configuration.

| Configuration | Backbone | Req. Acc. | Comment Acc. | Task Acc. | Company Acc. | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Codex | gpt-6.1-sol | 22.13% | 14.84% | 4.74% | 0.00% | $3,498.82 |
| Claude Code | claude-opus-5.5 | 21.18% | 14.21% | 4.21% | 0.00% | $3,713.03 |
| mini-SWE-agent | gpt-6.1-sol | 21.99% | 14.61% | 4.47% | 0.00% | $1,353.33 |
| mini-SWE-agent | claude-opus-5.5 | 23.74% | 16.37% | 5.26% | 0.00% | $2,327.73 |
| mini-SWE-agent | gemini-4-argon | 21.10% | 14.31% | 4.21% | 0.00% | $1,028.53 |
| mini-SWE-agent | deepseek-v4.1-flash | 19.56% | 12.55% | 3.42% | 0.00% | $392.47 |
| FilingLoop–Fixed | gpt-6.1-sol | 24.28% | 16.90% | 3.95% | 1.04% | $2,380.15 |
| FilingLoop–Evolving | gpt-6.1-sol | 27.96% | 20.75% | 5.00% | 2.08% | $2,519.23 |

**Evolution works, modestly.** FilingLoop–Evolving beats FilingLoop–Fixed on requirement fulfillment, 24.28% → 27.96% (+3.68 percentage points; 224 additional fulfilled requirements, +15.1% relative), and on full comment resolution, 16.90% → 20.75% (+3.85 points; 116 additional comments, +22.8% relative). Evolving also posts the highest Requirement and Comment Accuracies of any configuration, +4.22 and +4.38 points over the strongest external baseline (mini-SWE-agent with claude-opus-5.5). The workflow alone contributes independently of evolution: with the same gpt-6.1-sol backbone, Fixed exceeds Codex by 2.15 points and mini-SWE-agent by 2.29 points of Requirement Accuracy.

**The gains sit in revision, not discovery.** Reviewer discovery improves only slightly under evolution — requirement discovery 35.45% → 36.86% (+1.41 points), comment discovery 32.64% → 33.93%. On the shared Fixed target pool, conditional Filer Requirement Accuracy rises 68.50% → 78.86% (+10.36 points) and conditional Comment Accuracy 51.78% → 63.58% (+11.80 points). Procedural learning, as measured here, mostly teaches the Filer to close out concerns already found.

**Discovery is the bottleneck.** Under Evolving, 3,851 requirements remain undiscovered against 543 discovered but unfulfilled: the undiscovered group accounts for 87.6% of all remaining unfulfilled requirements. Even perfect revision of everything the Reviewer surfaced would cap system Requirement Accuracy at 36.86% — the discovery rate itself.

**Absolute performance is low.** Every configuration completes at least 99.21% of scheduled tasks, yet none exceeds 5.26% Task Accuracy (mini-SWE-agent with claude-opus-5.5; Evolving reaches 5.00%). At company level, Evolving fully resolves exactly 2 of 96 companies across all their review rounds; Fixed resolves 1, and every external configuration resolves zero. Execution reliability is effectively solved in this setup; review quality is not.

**Cost.** Evolving costs $2,519.23 across the stream versus Fixed's $2,380.15 (+5.8%), with recorded agent time up 3.1% (455.04 → 469.36 hours). Because outcome gains outrun the cost increase, cost per fulfilled requirement falls 8.1%, from $1.61 to $1.48 — $0.62 per additional fulfilled requirement on this stream. Evolving is also 28.0% cheaper than Codex and 32.2% cheaper than Claude Code at higher accuracy; the cheapest configuration overall, mini-SWE-agent with deepseek-v4.1-flash at $392.47, is also the least accurate (19.56%). Costs are computed from recorded usage against a saved price schedule — a usage-based total, as the authors note, rather than a reconciled invoice.

**Harm.** Among tasks with completed edit audits (coverage 99.74% for Fixed, 100% for Evolving), the harmful-revision rate is 5.8% for Fixed versus 4.5% for Evolving — a 1.3-point difference in task-level prevalence, with severity not quantified.

## Limitations

- **One model wears four hats.** gpt-6.1-sol is simultaneously the FilingLoop backbone, the grader, the bounded fact service's matcher, and the skill editor (it also annotated the annual-report extension). A grader from the same model family may systematically favor its own outputs' style and judgments in the matching and fulfillment stages. Using one grader for all configurations controls cross-configuration fairness; it does not control this bias, and its size is unknown. (Analyst-assessed; the paper discloses the shared model but does not analyze the circularity.)
- **Human–grader agreement is unquantified.** The authors state this directly: the evaluator was manually reviewed, and mechanical validation "does not establish semantic correctness." Every absolute number on this page inherits that gap.
- **The reference labels are model-annotated.** Ground truth was produced by one frontier model and audited by another, with team verification asserted but no measured agreement rate. Both the target labels and the scoring therefore carry model judgment; "27.96%" should not be read as a human-verified resolution rate.
- **A single seed and a single stream order.** There is no variance estimate and no confidence interval on the +3.68-point evolution gain, and batch-composition effects are untested. There is also no held-out test set: the authors describe the protocol as online adaptation with historical scoring feedback, earlier rounds of a company may inform its later rounds, and generalization to unseen companies is not established.
- **Contamination and corpus skew.** The reconstruction controls what agents can inspect, not what a model encountered in pretraining; the public filing and correspondence text may have appeared in training data. The corpus is concentrated on IPO registration review — the 2 annual-report tasks do not establish broader coverage — and historical letters record the concerns staff actually raised, not every defensible concern.
- **Requirements are occurrences, not independent observations.** Recurring concerns across a company's rounds are related, so the 6,099 denominator overstates the effective sample.
- **Leakage filters are incomplete.** The skill-content filters reject known identifiers and direct reference copying but, by the authors' own statement, cannot guarantee that all paraphrased case information is excluded from learned guidance.
- **Joint treatment, no ablation.** Both roles' skills update together, so attributing the system gain requires role-specific update ablations that were not performed; the Filer diagnostic in particular does not isolate the effect of updating the Filer alone.
- **Selection toward the reconstructable.** Eligibility requires cases identifiable from permitted records with evidence-supported requirements, limiting conclusions about matters needing unrecorded information or negotiation. Framework comparisons also entangle each baseline's native tool loop and expenditure with backbone identity — nominal step budgets do not establish equal inference expenditure.
- **The harm result is weak evidence.** A 1.3-point prevalence difference with no severity weighting and no significance analysis shows evolution did not obviously increase harm; it does not show evolution reduces it.

## Competing approaches

The evaluated baselines are Codex (gpt-6.1-sol), Claude Code (claude-opus-5.5), and mini-SWE-agent under four backbones (gpt-6.1-sol, claude-opus-5.5, gemini-4-argon, deepseek-v4.1-flash), all run by the authors through role adapters rather than taken from vendor-published figures; FilingLoop–Fixed is the internal control — the same workflow with skill updates disabled. The paper positions financial question-answering benchmarks (FinQA, TAT-QA, ConvFinQA, FinanceBench, BizBench, FinBen, the Finance Agent Benchmark) as evaluating answers *about* filings, not the review-and-revise loop FilingLoop measures. Among self-improvement methods — Self-Refine, Reflexion, ExpeL, Voyager, Agent Workflow Memory — and prompt or structure optimizers — APE, OPRO, ProTeGi, TextGrad, GEPA, ADAS, AFlow — the paper's stated distinction is that it does not compare prompt-search algorithms at all: it applies procedural reflection driven by evidence-linked, requirement-level feedback to both professional roles inside a fixed workflow, against a matched fixed-policy control.

## Implications

The clearest signal is where the ceiling sits. For review-type agent work — auditing a large artifact against an external party's concerns — the binding constraint measured here is omission-finding, not response quality: an agent that never notices a problem cannot fix it, and 87.6% of the residual failure is exactly that. Investment in discovery (systematic coverage strategies, independent second-pass reviewers, checklist generation) should dominate investment in revision polish until the discovery rate moves. Second, the evolution protocol is a reusable design for learning from production feedback without contaminating measurement: batch the stream, freeze skills until both arms are scored, lock first-attempt results before releasing feedback, validate updates structurally rather than by held-out score, and bind each case to the guidance in force when it started. That discipline is what makes the Fixed-versus-Evolving comparison interpretable at all, and it transfers to any setting where an agent system improves from its own graded history — the concerns catalogued under [[techniques/evaluation-and-simulation]] apply with full force. Third, gains concentrated in the role whose feedback is most concrete and checkable (the Filer's, where fulfillment is verifiable against the filing) suggest procedural learning pays first where success criteria are mechanical. Finally, the benchmark itself is a template: professional review correspondence in public archives can be reconstructed into interactive tasks whose hidden references supply requirement-level ground truth, a pattern applicable well beyond securities filings.

## Reproducible experiment

A reader can reproduce the *design* from the paper's appendices, which specify construction, environment, prompts, the evolution protocol, metrics, and settings; no public code or data repository accompanies the paper (see Evidence status), so reproduction means reimplementation, not rerunning released artifacts. The minimal version: (1) From EDGAR, select IPO registration filings with complete comment-letter correspondence; reconstruct one review round per task, decomposing each staff comment into atomic requirements with dispositions, and sequester the letters, replies, and amendments as the hidden reference. (2) Implement the two-role loop with any capable backbone: a Reviewer whose comments must carry quotations matching the permitted documents, a Filer restricted to the four response types and find/replace edits, a host that commits only uniquely matching edits, and a fact service that releases only evidence-supported facts matched to stated concerns. (3) Grade frozen outputs with a separate model in the three stages (matching, fulfillment, audit), with denominators fixed before execution and undiscovered concerns retained. (4) Run Fixed and Evolving arms over one seeded, company-interleaved stream in batches of at most 16, freezing skills until both arms are scored, then letting an editor update each role's skill from locked feedback under validate-or-keep. The quantities to compare are Requirement Accuracy, conditional Filer accuracy on the Fixed pool, the undiscovered share of residual failures, and cost per fulfilled requirement. Expect the discovery ceiling — not the evolution delta — to be the first thing a replication confirms or refutes.

## Evidence status

Every quantitative claim on this page is author-reported: one research group, its own benchmark, a single seeded stream, and a single grading model that also serves as the framework's backbone, fact matcher, and skill editor. The primary source (arXiv:2610.37905 v1, full text including Appendices A–I) was read in full on 2026-11-02 for the issue #2 upgrade of this page; the numbers above were checked against its tables and text at that time. No independent replication exists — the only secondary coverage found restates the abstract. The paper implies an accompanying implementation (it references shared prompts and build scripts) but publishes no code or data URL, and a web search on 2026-11-02 found no official repository; the underlying records are public SEC EDGAR documents. Nothing on this page rests on executing the system or reproducing the benchmark locally.
